## Purpose

Q Wealth is introducing artificial intelligence (AI) tools — including Claude, built by Anthropic — to help our team work more efficiently. This policy sets out how we use AI responsibly, so that we protect our clients, our reputation, and our obligations as an Authorised Representative operating under AFSL 543 023.

Regulators have made clear that using AI does not reduce our accountability. ASIC's review of AI adoption across the industry found that many licensees had not yet put AI-specific governance in place, and reiterated that we remain fully responsible for the quality of any advice or communication we rely on — regardless of what tool helped produce it. ASIC and APRA have since jointly reinforced this expectation, particularly around cyber resilience. This policy is our response to that expectation.

This policy exists to help us:

- Work faster, without cutting corners
- Keep client information secure and confidential
- Know exactly which AI tools we can use, and for what
- Meet ASIC, APRA and OAIC expectations for AI governance

## Scope

This policy applies to:

- Everyone who works at Q Wealth — partners, advisers, paraplanners and support staff — regardless of employment type
- Any generative AI or large language model tool used for Q Wealth business, whether a firm-provided account (such as our Claude subscription) or a personal one
- Any device — office computer, laptop or mobile — used to access AI tools for work purposes

This policy doesn't replace our existing Cyber Security Policy, Privacy Policy, Best Interests Policy, or Breach and Incident Management Policy — it sits alongside them. Where those policies set a stricter requirement, that requirement applies.

## Our Approach to AI

Five principles guide everything else in this policy:

- **Human-Centric by design.** AI powers the relationship between us and the client. It doesn't replace it.
- **AI is a tool, not a decision-maker.** It helps our people work better — it doesn't replace their judgement.
- **A human is always accountable.** Every team member remains fully responsible for their work, exactly as if no AI tool had been involved.
- **Client trust comes first.** We never trade client confidentiality, or our best interests duty, for speed.
- **We adopt deliberately.** New AI tools are reviewed and approved before they're used — not after.

## Key Definitions

- **Artificial Intelligence (AI):** technology that performs tasks normally requiring human intelligence — writing, summarising, analysing, or answering questions.
- **Generative AI:** AI that creates new content in response to a prompt, such as Claude or ChatGPT.
- **Approved AI tool:** an AI tool Q Wealth has reviewed and formally approved, and added to our approved application register (see our Cyber Security Policy).
- **Client information:** anything that identifies, or could reasonably identify, a client — name, contact details, TFN, account numbers, balances, health information, or anything drawn from a fact find, SOA or file note.

## What AI Can Help With

Used well, AI is a strong first-draft assistant. Good uses include:

- Drafting first-draft correspondence, meeting summaries and file notes — for you to check and finalise
- Researching markets, the economy, superannuation and tax settings (always confirm anything time-sensitive against a primary source)
- Summarising long documents, reports or email threads
- Brainstorming client communications, newsletters and presentations
- Building and checking spreadsheets, formulas and data analysis
- Coding and automating internal tools and processes
- Preparing internal training material, checklists and templates

## What AI Must Not Be Used For

Regardless of which tool you're using, do not:

- Enter client-identifying information, TFNs, account numbers, passwords or health information into any AI tool that isn't on our approved list
- Rely on AI-generated content as final advice, a Statement of Advice, or client-ready communication without a qualified team member reviewing, checking and taking ownership of it
- Let AI make — or effectively make — a financial product recommendation on its own. Our best interests duty and advice process must always be applied, and owned, by a human adviser
- Treat AI-generated compliance, legal or tax commentary as authoritative — always verify with a qualified professional
- Connect any AI tool to our CRM, email or file storage without approval
- Use AI to skip or shortcut our usual fact-finding, advice or record-keeping steps

## Q Wealth Business Use Only

Our Claude and Microsoft Copilot accounts are licensed and paid for by Q Wealth, for Q Wealth work. They are not for personal use, and they are not for any other business.

- Use these accounts only for Q Wealth client work, research, and internal operations
- Do not use them for personal tasks unrelated to your role at Q Wealth
- Do not use them for any other business, employer, side venture, directorship, or unrelated work — including work for family, friends, or other organisations you're involved with
- If you're ever unsure whether something counts as Q Wealth business, ask your manager or our AI Policy Owner first

This isn't about limiting how much you use AI — it's about making sure the tools we pay for, and the data protections that come with them, are used for the purpose they're approved for.

## Responsible Use of AI Capacity

Claude and Copilot both draw on usage capacity behind the scenes. We want the team to use AI freely and thoroughly wherever it genuinely helps — this section is common sense, not a hard limit.

- Use clear, well-scoped prompts, and iterate rather than re-running very large requests over and over for the same task
- Avoid leaving sessions running unnecessarily, or reaching for AI on trivial tasks that don't really need it
- Don't hold back on tasks that genuinely need it. Thorough client work, research and analysis are exactly what these tools are for — conservation should never come at the cost of quality or thoroughness where it matters.
- If you notice unusually heavy usage patterns — yours or someone else's — mention it to our AI Policy Owner. It's not a compliance issue, just good housekeeping so there's plenty of capacity for everyone

## Client Data, Confidentiality & Privacy

Treat every AI conversation the way you'd treat an email or a client file — because from a confidentiality standpoint, it's exactly the same kind of record.

- Our approved Claude plan does not use our conversations to train Anthropic's models, and comes with enterprise-level data protections — but this doesn't remove our own obligations under the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles
- Only use approved tools (see Approved AI Tools) for anything touching client or firm-confidential information
- Any new AI tool proposed for use with client data must go through the same vendor and security review as any other supplier, and be added to our approved application and asset register
- The Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC) has published guidance on using commercial AI products responsibly — this policy reflects that guidance for our size and risk profile

## Human Oversight & Professional Accountability

*AI drafts. People decide.*

Every AI-assisted output — an email, a summary, a piece of analysis — must be reviewed by a suitably qualified team member before it is relied on, sent to a client, or forms part of an advice document. This is not optional, and it cannot be delegated to the tool.

Advisers remain fully accountable for anything presented to a client, including every step of the Best Interests Duty (the 'Safe Harbour') — this obligation cannot be delegated to an AI tool, however capable it seems.

- If you can't explain how a conclusion was reached, don't use it
- Applying healthy scepticism to AI output is simply good professional practice — it isn't a sign of distrust in the tool

## Checking AI Output for Accuracy

AI tools can sound completely confident while being wrong — this is sometimes called a 'hallucination'. An AI tool might invent a piece of legislation, a tax rate, a case, or a product feature that doesn't exist.

- Always verify figures, dates, legislative and tax references, and product details against a primary or firm-approved source
- Never assume an AI tool has access to the latest rates, thresholds or legislation — check anything time-sensitive
- Where a client's personal circumstances are involved, verify against our own records, not the AI's summary of them

## Incidents & Breach Reporting

If you suspect that client or firm-confidential information has been entered into a non-approved AI tool, or that an AI tool has produced or disclosed information inappropriately, report it immediately — do not wait, and don't worry about looking overly cautious.

This is treated the same way as any other incident under our Breach and Incident Management Policy and Data Breach Policy. Early reporting is what protects our clients and the firm — waiting rarely helps.
