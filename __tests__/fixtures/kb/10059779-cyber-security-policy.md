## Context

The purpose of this document is to outline the minimum security policies and measures that are recommended to be maintained by Authorised Representatives of Financial Advice Co (FAC) in order to ensure that risks associated with data management and security are well managed so that data breaches do not occur and are minimised. You are required to use this document to formulate a data security policy that works for your practice. A checklist to assist you is attached at Appendix 3.

## What is Cyber Security

Cyber security comprises of the steps applied during the creation, acquisition, processing, transmission, storage, and disposal of digital or electrical information in order to protect networks, devices and programs and data from attack, damage or unauthorised access. Cyber security is essentially information technology security.

As client information is collected and handled by advisers, staff, contractors, and suppliers; the application and monitoring of appropriate information security controls are required to keep the information safe.

## Why is it important?

Information theft and identity theft, have become an everyday occurrence resulting in unauthorised external access through cyber hacking or phishing, to gain internal access to sensitive data caused by inadequate security systems and protocols. All employees and third-party users should be aware of their responsibilities in protecting client information and be able to report any potential breaches.

## What is a data breach

The Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC) is a government body charged with responsibility for the Privacy act. Included in their scope is ensuring transparency when data breaches occur.

A data breach happens when personal information is accessed or disclosed without authorisation, or that information is lost. The legislation requires that you must notify affected individuals and the OAIC when a data breach involving personal information is likely to result in serious harm. This policy is designed to help you implement data security for your practice, so that you can minimise the risk of a data breach occurring, and most importantly protect your client’s personal identifiable data.

Please refer to the Data Breach Policy and Procedures which explains what personal information and sensitive personal information is and outlines the breach reporting obligations. In the first instance if you do have a data breach please contact [contact@financialadviceco.com.au](mailto:contact@financialadviceco.com.au) immediately and we will provide instructions on next steps, including; potentially notifying clients impacted, remediation and, if assessed as reportable, reporting the data breach to the Australian Information Commissioner.

## Annual checklist

## Key elements of cyber security guidance

The key elements of an effective Cyber security policy include the following key areas:

### Security awareness

A critical component of the identification of security threats, is to ensure that all staff within your practice are trained and made aware of the various elements of Cyber Security. You must ensure staff are trained (and provided with ongoing awareness training) about data security, email attacks and our policies and procedures.

### Clear Desk and Clear Screen

- Steps must be taken to ensure that all printed information is protected from unauthorised view when their desk is left unattended for extended periods and removed from their desk and secured when the desk is unoccupied at the end of the workday.
- Computers are recommended to be ‘locked’ when left unattended for extended periods (i.e., by using the Windows + L keyboard combination) to prevent unauthorised access to information.
- Businesses should implement a universal lock-screen policy for when a user is inactive on their device, that the machine locks itself after ten minutes.

### Passwords

Passwords are your first line of defence. Therefore, the challenge is to ensure that they are robust and secure. The following parameters are recommended:

- Minimum length 15 Characters or a phrase
- Last 12 passwords remembered – this means you cannot reuse the last 12 passwords
- Multi Factor Authentication must be enabled on all accounts
- Must contain 3 of the following 5 categories:
  - Uppercase characters of Roman alphabet (A-Z).
  - Lowercase characters of Roman alphabet (a-z)
  - Digits (0-9)
  - Non-Alphanumeric characters (~!@#$%^&\*\_-+=\`|\\(){}\[\]:;"'\<\>,.?/)
  - Any unicode character, includes non-Roman Alphabets.
- Account lockout after 5 failed login attempts
- Computer lock to be applied after 10 minutes of inactivity, requiring a user to re-enter their password upon returning.
- Passwords are NOT to be shared. Any access must be granted through an approved mechanism.
- Passwords are not to be displayed or physically stored and/or concealed on your workspace
- Passwords may not contain:
  - All or part of the user's account name
  - First name, middle name, or last name
  - Company name
  - Any portion of your Medicare number
  - Any portion of your address
  - Any portion of your date of birth
  - Nickname
  - Any term that could easily be guessed by someone who is familiar with you
- Passwords should be enabled on Desktops, Laptops, Tablets and Smartphones to protect client data in the event of theft or loss.
- Electronic information stored on discs, USB and portable drives should be password protected.

A password manager solution **must** be used and may assist in user adoption of password usage, through features such as the ability to generate and store random passwords to strengthen password effectiveness. E.g. Last Pass, Bit Warden, Keeper or Dashlane. Poor, or weak, passwords have the following characteristics:

- Contain less than 15 characters.
- Can be found in a dictionary, including foreign language, or exist in a language slang, dialect, or jargon.
- Contain personal information such as birthdates, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, or names of family members, pets, friends, and fantasy characters.
- Contain work-related information such as building names, system commands, sites, companies, hardware, or software.
- Contain number patterns such as aaabbb, qwerty, zyxwvuts, or 123321.
- Contain common words spelled backward, or preceded or followed by a number (for example, terces, secret1 or 1secret).
- Are some version of “Welcome123” “Password123” “Changeme123”

- Passwords must not be recorded physically, or electronically in an unencrypted form.
- Passwords should be created in a manner that facilitates recall. This may be accomplished by basing passwords on a song title, affirmation, or other phrase. For example, the phrase, "This May Be One Way To Remember" could become the password *TmB1w2R!* or a variation. (NOTE: Do not use either of these examples as passwords!)

### Endpoint detection (Anti Virus)

Endpoint detection is the modern version of anti-virus software which protects your computers and data from viruses and cyber-attacks. Today’s latest technologies go further than traditional anti-virus solutions and can even roll back ransomware attacks. You must have Endpoint detection (Anti-Virus) software installed on all business laptops (devices).

The following antivirus settings are recommended:

- On access virus scanning enabled
- Periodic scans of all files including compressed files
- Set to automatically clean and / or quarantine
- Block known malicious websites through firewall software. Scan downloaded content from websites
- Detect and alert suspicious behaviour and traffic
- Allow for reporting of disabled and out of date antivirus software

### Use multi factor authentication

Multi factor authentication (MFA) is used to ensure that users are who they say they are by requiring that they provide at least two pieces of evidence to prove their identity. Each piece of evidence must come from a different category: something they know, something they have or something they are. The most common examples of multi factor authentication in use are third party authenticators or one-time codes sent to a verified email address or phone number.

The use of MFA must be used on applications that hold any client information e.g. XPLAN, CRM, banking websites, email systems and potentially even social media. It adds an additional layer of protection to ensure that even if your password is stolen, your data stays protected.

### Client Transactions or Requests to Change Accounts

Do not accept transaction requests via email alone, this includes requests to change nominated bank accounts. Any transactions or requests to change nominated bank accounts must be verified by the client directly using a known phone number. Do not send a text message, instead call your client directly and discuss the transaction. Further, check the authenticity of the client’s instructions (e.g. use the phone number on the client file and not the one in the email, check signatures, verify correct email address), verify identity for telephone instructions. Where information is jointly owned, you should attain verification from both parties acting on the instructions.

### Mobile device security

Ensure you and your team only use authorised devices when connecting to your systems. Use MFA to verify identity when logging into company systems and consider mobile security systems if sensitive systems are accessed from mobile devices. Public WiFi services should not be used; use of a hotspot facility incorporated in user’s smartphone is preferred.

### Firewalls

A firewall is a security device — computer hardware or software — that can help protect your network by filtering traffic and blocking outsiders from gaining unauthorised access to the private data on your computer.

Ensure your firewall is maintained and turn on intrusion detection and prevention features to minimise the risk of unauthorised access occurring.

### Encryption

Encrypt sensitive documents so that only authorised users can access company information. Where possible send your clients a link to a secure system when sending out information containing personal information, this way, even if the email is intercepted, only an authorised person with access to a secure system can access the data. Passwords should not be communicated together with the link.

The use of a corporate VPN is recommended for remote users when connecting from remote locations. This provides secure encrypted network access. Personal VPN usage, however, is prohibited (e.g. NordVPN, ExpressVPN, IPVanish etc.) as these services have a history of being hacked.

- Enable full disk encryption on your computer or laptop
- Enable encryption on your mobile devices
- Ensure data is encrypted on portable storage devices
- Encrypting email file attachments

### Approved Applications

All software will have bugs and security vulnerabilities that will require continual patching to reduce the risks to client information. A security vulnerability could result in your computer being compromised and a malware installed to steal or destroy client information. Reducing the amount of software will reduce the risks, and maintenance required to protect client information.

Only approved applications required for business purposes should be installed and used on your work computer. An asset and application register is required be maintained as this may be requested for review and assessment.

### Patching

Security vulnerabilities within operating systems and applications are continually being identified and updated with patches. It is important that these patches are installed as soon as possible to protect your device and information from malware and ransomware.

- Ensure you keep your main systems updated and avoid using outdated systems (e.g. windows XP). We recommend that you set your key operating systems such as Microsoft, Apple and Adobe to update automatically. This ensures that whenever your software needs updating it happens without you needing to think about it.
- Operating systems should have security updates installed within 30 days of becoming available.
- Microsoft Office Updates should be installed within 30 days of becoming available.
- Web browsers – Google Chrome, Firefox, Internet Explorer, Edge, Safari are recommended to be set to automatically update.
- PDF editing / reading software should be set to automatically update.

### Disable Microsoft Office Macros From The Internet

Microsoft Office documents containing malicious macros are a common delivery method for malware. Macros from the internet are recommended not be downloaded.

### Disposal of Storage Devices

When a computer or storage device that has contained or could have contained critical and/or sensitive information is no longer required, care must be taken when repurposing or disposing of the device.

These devices must be securely wiped (not just deleted) or the storage media (e.g., internal / portable hard drive, USB flash drive, backup tape etc) physically destroyed, so any information cannot be recovered, before being repurposed or disposed of.

### Back up

Put in place multi backup systems:

- Back up your data locally and to the cloud (securely).
- Backups should take place at least daily.
- Backup data should be available over a 7-year timespan.
- Have an offline back up for each month of the year. Backups should be tested at least annually.

### Network

Do not use public computers or public Wi-fi to access your practice email account or systems. If you are travelling or going out of the office for meetings and require Wi-Fi, use your mobile phone’s personal hotspot.

### Acceptable use

FAC proprietary information stored on electronic and computing devices, whether owned or leased by FAC, the employee or a third party, remains the sole property of FAC.

You must ensure through legal and/or technical means that proprietary information is protected in accordance with the Data Breach Policy and Procedure.

- You have a responsibility to promptly report the theft, loss, or unauthorised access of proprietary or confidential information.
- You may access FAC proprietary information only to the extent it is authorised.
- All team members are responsible for exercising good judgment regarding the reasonableness of personal use.

The guidance includes acceptable uses for your practices:

- Proprietary information stored on electronic and computing devices, whether owned or leased by yourselves, an employee or a third party, remains the property of the practice.
- All employees, contractors and staff must promptly report theft, loss or unauthorised disclosure of proprietary or confidential information.
- Employees, contractors and staff must only access proprietary information to the extent it is authorised and necessary to fulfil their duties.
- Employees are responsible for exercising good judgment regarding the reasonableness of personal use.
- Corporate systems may be used for reasonable personal use whilst at work, but accessing of user’s material that is generally considered to be not socially acceptable, is not permitted on company infrastructure.
- Only approved software is to be installed on any corporate device. This may be re-enforced via an automated technical solution.

### Cyber Insurance

Cyber Insurance is designed to help your organisation mitigate risk exposure by offsetting costs involved with recovery after a cyber related security breach or a similar event.

Please discuss your needs with insurance brokers to ensure you are adequately covered. It is a requirement that you **must** have Cyber practice insurance in place and coverage incorporating these key risk areas:

- Investigation and remediation expenses; which includes the costs associated with investigation and identification of what data has been breached; how it has been breached and how it can be remediated
- Legal Expenses, which includes costs associated with obtaining advice to manage instances of data breaches. You may also need legal representation in the event that affected clients initiate legal action
- Notification Expenses; which includes direct costs of notification (e.g. staff, stationery, mail outs; etc)
- Regulatory Fines and Penalties: In some instances, you may be liable for costs of regulatory enforcement
- Credit Monitoring and ID Theft Repair. This may not be legally required, but if you are able to offer these services to your clients it may reduce your potential liability
- Public Relations Expenses. Significant breaches can severely damage your reputation, so this must be managed carefully
- Make sure your policies cover staff working outside of your primary place of business, including from home or offshore.

## Email and Communications

- Never open e-mail attachments received from unknown senders, or unexpected or unusual emails from known senders, as these may contain malware.
- Emails relating to the business must not be sent via 3rd party emails providers such Hotmail, gmail etc.
- Emails with attachments that contain any personally identifiable, or financial client information must be password protected. Passwords must never be sent by email and should be communicated via an alternate mechanism such as text, or verbally. We strongly recommend that you commence using web based tools such as client portals. We have a number of these solutions that will integrate to existing advice software.

## Physical security

- Building and office premises must be locked when no staff are in the office
- All computing devices must be secured with a password-protected screensaver with the automatic activation feature set to 10 minutes or less. The computer must be locked or logged off when the device is unattended.
- Laptops and easily removable devices are locked to desks i.e. Kensington locks.
- Enforce clean desk and clean screen policy (password protected screen saver) to mitigate the risk of unauthorised access beyond work hours
- Must maintain an IT asset register for all devices issued by the practice including device type, name of person device is issued to, device model, date issued, serial number and warranty information.

## Unacceptable Use

When using company resources to access email and the internet to communicate, users must realise they represent your business as well as your Licensee.

Users must at all times comply with the law, and social norms, including (but not limited to):

- Laws against workplace harassment or bullying;
- Laws against discrimination including sexual orientation, political persuasion, union affiliation and gender.
- Compliance with anti-hawking provisions around unsolicited emails;
- Using company accounts to express political or personal views or opinions;
- Representing that you are communicating on behalf of your business where they are not authorised to do so.

## Third party risk management

When using third party suppliers and partners you must ensure that these are regularly assessed to guarantee compliance with required security standards. This also includes the use of third party providers for outsourcing and offshoring purposes.

When dealing with outsourcing and offshore providers, you must ensure that all client information is only transferred through secure means as identified in this policy.

## Appendix 1

Based on the five-stage National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Framework, this plan is set out in three parts:

### What to do before any cybersecurity breach (Identify and Protect)

- Identify where you are now, what your risks and exposures are, this is your ‘needs analysis’ stage. Need to consider where the gaps may be in order to protect your client’s (and staff’s) personal data.
- Once you have identified the gaps then, you will need to put in place the IT and security needed to protect your client’s data.

### What to do at the time of a breach (Detect and Respond)

- Awareness, training and systems in place to detect attacks.
- Know how to respond immediately as timing is critical

### What to do after (Recover, Repair and Rectify)

- You must have a plan in place to recover, repair and rectify vulnerabilities. Your plan will require resources and effort. There will be reporting involved potentially to FAC, clients, regulators and any suppliers.
- Post an event or an attack, there will be learnings and further actions to implement.
