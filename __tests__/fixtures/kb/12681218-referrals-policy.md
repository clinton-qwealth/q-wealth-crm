## What this policy covers

The policy provides an overview of the disclosure and other regulatory requirements when referring a client to other service providers, and when providing advice to a client that has been referred to you.

## General Principles

### Conflicts of Interest

The Financial Adviser Standards and Ethics Authority (FASEA) has published guidance in relation to Conflicts of Interest, in relation to referrals.

FASEA standard 3, in particular is relevant and says *“You must not advise, refer or act in any other manner where you have a conflict of interest or duty”.*

Management of this matter is particularly pertinent where you have interests and associations with a related business (e.g. tax, accounting, SMSF administration, and the like). Such interests and associations will **always** be an actual conflict of interest.

### How do I effectively manage this?

ASIC has noted that they are taking a “facilitative approach” to the implementation of FASEA guidance. Financial Advice Co is approaching this issue in the following manner:

- When making or receiving a referral where there is an actual or potential conflict of interest you must be able to show that acting on that referral is ***demonstrably*** in your client’s best interests. This requires a higher level of evidence than simply being in the client’s best interests. You must document in your client’s file:
  - Reasons why this arrangement is preferable to other reasonable alternatives.
  - Evidence of explanation of other alternatives and why they are not suitable.

You may make a referral payment to a third party (who is not an Authorised Representative) in these circumstances. Payments can be made to the individual referrer or the referrer’s corporate entity. For the avoidance of doubt where the referrer is an authorised representative of any licensee, payments may not be made to the individual and must always be made to a corporate entity.

### What are the golden rules?

When making or receiving a referral where there is an actual or potential conflict of interest, then the following steps must be followed:

- Can the conflict be avoided? If it can be avoided then it should.
- If it cannot be avoided then you must disclose and obtain client’s consent in writing to acting despite the conflict, and demonstrate why acting in this manner is demonstrably in your client’s best interest. This includes satisfying yourself that the required services can be provided competently and establishing that fees and charges are fair and reasonable and represent value for money.
- If you cannot demonstrate that a referral is demonstrably in the client’s best interests when referring to a party where there is a conflict of interest, **then you must not refer to that party**.

### Due diligence

In order to demonstrate that a referral to a referral partner is in the best interests of your client, you need to have evidence on file of the due diligence you have conducted. An outbound referral partner due diligence checklist is attached at Appendix 1. This should be completed and retained on file for all new referral partners.

### Can I receive a fee for a referral?

You cannot receive a fee in your capacity as an individual Authorised Representative. You can receive a fee in the Corporate capacity.

Standard 7 of the FASEA Code of Conduct states: *“… you may not receive any benefits, in connection with acting for a client, that derive from a third party other than your principal.”*

## Referrals Out

An outbound referral arrangement occurs when you provide a recommendation to a client to consult with a third party (for example, a solicitor, or accountant), and this referral is in relation to the overall advice being provided to your client. Where you make such a referral, you must advise your client, in writing, details of:

- The referral you have made;
- The purpose of the referral;
- Any fee or commissions that a related party (e.g. Corporate Authorised Representative) will or is likely to receive in relation to making that referral.
- If there is any conflict of interest, how that is demonstrably in your client’s best interests.

Additionally, note that:

- You may not charge a fee to a client for making a referral.
- You must obtain the client’s consent in writing to pass contact or any other information to a third party.

As noted in the Due diligence section above, the Outbound Referrals Due Diligence checklist needs to be completed to evidence the suitability of outbound referral partners.

## Referrals to a credit provider

Additional obligations apply where you make a referral to an Authorised Credit Provider. These are:

- you must provide the referral as a matter that is incidental to carrying on another business;
- you must give the consumer’s name and contact details to the licensee or representative within five business days.

## Referrals In

Inbound referrals relate to referrals by third party referral partners to a Financial Advice Co representative.

Where you receive such a referral, you must disclose in your advice documents:

- The fact that you have received such a referral;
- Any fee that you pay to the third party in relation to making that referral.

Where you enter into an inbound referral arrangement, please discuss with compliance to ensure the appropriate information is collected so that the Financial Advice Co operations team can populate the referral details in the referral agreement, where required. Your compliance manager will advise you if a formal referral agreement is required.
