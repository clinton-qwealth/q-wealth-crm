This policy is a subset of Financial Advice Co compliance framework, designed to ensure that Financial Advice Co and its Authorised Representatives meets its obligations under the law and act in a manner consistent with the provision of quality advice to its clients, and applies to those Authorised Representatives who are authorised by Financial Advice Co to provide full advice to retail clients.

## To whom does this policy apply

This policy applies to those Authorised Representatives whom Financial Advice Co have authorised to provide full advice under its AFSL. (Separate provisions apply to limited authority holders).

## Overview

There are a range of issues associated with providing advice to clients who are not physically in Australia. The primary issue is one of jurisdiction. Some countries, for example, require an adviser to be licensed in their jurisdiction even where advice is in relation to affairs not physically present in that jurisdiction.

Consequently, if advice is provided to a resident of a foreign jurisdiction then issues may arise as to liability, consumer protection and the like.

## Providing advice to Residents of a foreign jurisdiction

Because of the jurisdictional, licensing, consumer protection and liability issues, advice can only be provided to residents of a foreign jurisdiction in the following circumstances.

- Advice can only be provided to overseas residents when they have properly appointed Power of Attorney who must be physically in Australia in the event that we require documentation signed.
- Your advice must be limited to the client’s financial situation in Australia. An appropriate advice warning must be made to the effect that the advice is limited to their Australian affairs and does not take into account assets or liabilities held overseas.
- You cannot provide advice in respect of affairs in other countries, for the reasons noted above. Additionally, asset/liabilities/insurances held overseas are subject to different legal and taxation requirements to those that apply in Australia.
- Funds must be drawn from and paid into Australian bank accounts.
- AML/CTF processes must be completed for the client and the Power of Attorney, where applicable.
- All mail must be sent to an Australian address (but not the adviser’s or an associate of the adviser’s address).
- You cannot market to overseas jurisdictions.

## Australian Residents temporarily overseas

Many of your Australian clients may need advice when they are overseas. For example, clients on holidays, or working overseas.

We would expect that these would be existing clients with ongoing service obligations, not new clients.

In these cases you may provide advice to those clients only in respect of their Australian financial affairs. Communication and instructions may be over the phone, by video conferencing, or email. Any documents emailed should be appropriately secured by encryption or password.

Note that we require clients who are away for extended periods (more than 12 months) to appoint an Australian based Power of Attorney (who may not be you or your staff) to act on their behalf whilst they are away.

## Pension transfers

Many clients who have migrated to Australia have overseas superannuation or pension accounts which they may wish to transfer to Australia. Every overseas jurisdiction has different rules and regulations affecting taxation, eligible funds, amounts that can be transferred, and the manner in which they can be transferred.

Where you have such a client, and you wish to provide advice in this area you should discuss with the Financial Advice Co compliance team to determine whether you have the requisite knowledge to ensure that the transfer of such amounts is done in accordance with the regulations, before providing advice.

In the event that it is determined that you do not have the necessary skill or experience Financial Advice Co can recommend a referral to assist the client, or you may refer to another qualified referral partner.

## Best Interests obligations

In some instances, you may not be able to provide advice in the client’s best interests where you cannot take their overseas holdings into account. In these circumstances you must decline to provide advice and refer to an internationally based advisory practice who have representatives authorised to provide advice in both Australia and the relevant jurisdiction. Generally, the large internationally based accounting practices are best suited.

## Non-compliance with this policy

In addition to other specific measures referred to in this policy, non-compliance with this policy will be classified as an incident and will be dealt with as per the Breach and Incident Policy.
