## Background

The Financial Adviser Standards and Ethics Authority (FASEA) has been charged with implementing the Corporations Amendment (Professional Standards of Financial Advisers) Act 2017. The amendment makes changes to the Corporations Act 2001 to raise the education, training and ethical standards of financial advisers by requiring relevant providers to hold a degree (or higher or equivalent) qualification, pass an exam, undertake a professional year, undertake continuous professional development and comply with a Code.

Transitional arrangements exist for existing advisers.

This document deals with the Code of Ethics issued by FASEA, which is a set of principles and core values that lays the foundations for a true profession to emerge. It is a principles based model providing a powerful framework to shape and reinforce ethical conduct and encourage a deeper engagement by the individual with their duties to their client as well as wider society.

## How can I comply with the Code?

**Appendix 1** sets out detailed guidance on Financial Advice Co’s position regarding compliance with the Code. However, in principle, Financial Advice Co will consider that you are complying with the Code in circumstances where you:

- **Are demonstrably acting in the client’s best interests.**
This is a high standard to comply with and must be backed up with robust record keeping, fair and consistent fees, and documented client engagement processes to ensure your client understands the nature of the engagement, understands and accepts your advice and its limitations, and where you have considered the broad effects of the advice you have provided.
- **Are acting fairly, honestly and efficiently in your dealings with your clients.**
This means, always obeying the law, avoiding conflicts of interest, and charging your clients fairly for the services that you are providing. Where you cannot avoid a conflict, or don’t believe it is in your client’s best interest to do so, you must fully document the reasons why that conflict can’t be avoided as well as why that conflict is essential to you acting demonstrably in the client’s best interest.
- **Deliver the services that you have promised.**
Put in place properly documented Letters of Engagement for each new client, which sets out the terms and scope of your engagement, as well as services and anticipated fees. Letter of Engagement templates are available on the Adviser websites.
- **Take into account your client’s broad circumstances.**
This means that even if your client approaches you for limited advice, you are still accountable to consider all the circumstances in determining whether the limited advice is in your client’s best interests. Refer clients to other providers where you have conducted sufficient due diligence to satisfy yourself that the preferred referee is best suited for satisfying the needs of your client. You may also not receive any benefit as a consequence of referrals. Fees may be paid via your licensee or a Corporate Authorised Representative, but only in circumstances where you comply with standard 1 of this code – i.e., are not seeking to circumvent the provisions of this code.
- **You act in good faith and with competence, and maintain knowledge and skills.**
This means that you must remember and live the values of acting honestly, fairly and efficiently, maintaining accreditations and CPD obligations and must not give advice where in good faith you are not competent to do so.
- **Cooperate with ASIC.**
This means that you must efficiently and promptly, in consultation with your licensee, respond to requests from ASIC for information and/or documents.
- **Uphold your ethical standards.**
This means that you must cooperate within the ethical standards that we have published in our various policies, including the Adviser Responsibilities policy. Act collegiately for the good of all.

## Limited Scope Advice

Limited Scope engagements may only be undertaken where you have obtained sufficient information to determine that it is in your client’s best interests to provide limited scope advice.

This is because the client may not properly understand the consequences of deferring or scoping out aspects of advice.

In these circumstances:

- A full fact find setting out client’s current situation, needs, goals and objectives must still be conducted so that the client’s needs can be identified and the consequences of scoping out of certain areas of advice can be properly documented.

## Appendix 1: Overview of Standards and Financial Advice Co Guidance

### Standard 1: Compliance with the Law

You must act in accordance with all applicable laws, including this Code, and not try to avoid or circumvent their intent.

| **Key Components** | **Guidance** |
| --- | --- |
| **Obey the law** | Ensure you are up to date with all relevant advice policies published by Financial Advice Co. These reflect both the legal position and our business rules in relation to the provision of advice. We also publish short policy quizzes designed to guide you through the key components of our policies. Completing these will help demonstrate that you understand the law associated with provision of financial advice. |
| **Do not try to avoid or circumvent the law.** | Disclosure and client consent is not an acceptable solution to breaching the law or an advice policy. If you are acting in your clients’ best interests you will be unlikely to avoid or circumvent the law. Do not set up business structures and other arrangements that seek to avoid other standards of the code. |

### Standard 2: Best Interests

You must act with integrity and in the best interests of each of your clients.

| **Key Components** | **Guidance** |
| --- | --- |
| **Integrity** (which includes acting as a professional, keeping your promises and honouring your commitments) | Familiarise yourself with Financial Advice Co policy on adviser responsibilities, which has these values at its core. This includes: providing honest and complete information; not using client funds or information for your own benefit; not tolerating such behaviour in others; obeying the law; respecting confidentiality; ensuring you have systems in place to keep track of your obligations (e.g., diarise FDS and OSA obligations); and maintaining proper records of client interactions. |
| **Best Interests** | Familiarise yourself with our policy on Best Interests and complete the relevant quiz. If in doubt reach out and discuss with our compliance team. Make complete enquiries as the policy guidance requires you to “inquire more widely”. |
| **Inquire more widely into the client’s circumstances.** (Note you are not relieved of your ethical duty to enquire widely if your client declines to provide you with the information requested) | This requirement is embedded within the policy guidance. Therefore, in order to comply: you must complete a comprehensive fact find, even if your authority to provide advice is a limited authority; you must reflect your wide understanding of your client’s circumstances into your advice documents, and address them as part of your advice; if your client declines to provide enough information, even when asked, you should decline to provide advice; if your advice authority is limited you must still record these wide circumstances and make appropriate referral recommendations for your client to act upon (see Standard 7 re referral arrangements). |

### Standard 3: Conflicts of Interest

You must not advise, refer or act in any other manner where you have a conflict of interest or duty.

Financial Advice Co has taken the following approach to managing the ethical obligation of this standard, in line with RG 181 and aspects of standard 3 of the code.

| **Key Components** | **Guidance** |
| --- | --- |
| **Best Interest** | In the event there is a conflict of interest then provided it is *demonstrably* in the client’s best interest, then you may act, notwithstanding the conflict, provided that comprehensive evidence is documented showing that acting notwithstanding the conflict is demonstrably in your client’s best interest. |
| **Demonstrably** | Financial Advice Co view is that to act, notwithstanding a conflict that cannot be avoided requires a higher level of evidence than simply showing the advice is in the client’s best interests. This must include, and be appropriately documented: reasons why this arrangement is preferable to other reasonable alternatives; evidence of explanation of other alternatives and why they are not suitable. (In practical terms when considering other alternatives, you need to take into account ease of access by the client, costs and benefits). |
| **Related businesses** | The demonstrably in the client’s best interest test is particularly relevant when you operate a related business (e.g., accounting; tax advice; SMSF administration) etc. Options and explanations as to why your related business is demonstrably in your client’s best interests must be fully documented. |
| **Disclosure and consent** | If the conflict can be avoided then it should; if it can’t be avoided then you must disclose and obtain client’s consent to acting despite the conflict, and demonstrate why acting in this manner is demonstrably in your client’s best interest. |
| **Asset based or other variable fees** | The test for whether asset based (or other variable fees) may be charged is: would a disinterested person, in possession of all the facts, reasonably conclude that the form of variable income could induce an adviser to act in a manner inconsistent with the best interests of the client or the other provisions of the Code. Therefore, such fees must be properly documented to show that they are demonstrably in the client’s best interests. |

### Standard 4: Free Prior and Informed Consent

You may act for a client only with the client’s free, prior and informed consent. If required in the case of an existing client, the consent should be obtained as soon as practicable after this Code commences.

| **Key Components** | **Guidance** |
| --- | --- |
| **New client’s – pre advice** | Letters of Engagement (LoE) must be provided to all new clients. Templates are available on the adviser portal. At a minimum LoEs must cover: what services will be provided; the terms on which they will be provided; the records that will be made of the services (e.g. SoA, file notes, etc); privacy and confidentiality arrangements. |
| **Implementation** | Even after the client has agreed that you may provide the advice you must be satisfied that they understand what is being implemented, the risks and any limitations of the advice. Evidence of a signature on an ATP is not sufficient. You must document by way of file note the discussions and issues raised, as well as your client’s understanding and acceptance of limitations, risks and other relevant matters. |
| **Existing clients** | For existing clients subject to an Ongoing Service agreement, then the Ongoing Service Agreement will, subject to it containing the details outlined above, be sufficient to meet this obligation. |
| **Grandfathered clients** | Such clients must be contacted as soon as practicable to obtain their consent to continue to receive those payments. Dependent on the number of clients you should develop a plan that is practicable, ensuring that consent is received from all clients. If consent is not received these fees/commissions must be turned off. |

### Standard 5: Client Circumstances

All advice and financial product recommendations that you give to a client must be in the best interests of the client and appropriate to the client’s individual circumstances.

You must be satisfied that the client understands your advice, and the benefits, costs and risks of the financial products that you recommend, and you must have reasonable grounds to be satisfied.

| **Key Components** | **Guidance** |
| --- | --- |
| **Best Interests** | Ensure you are familiar with our Advice Policy on Best Interests. Ensure you document your adherence to the safe harbour steps contained in that policy. |
| **Appropriate to the client’s Individual circumstances** | Tailor your SoA to suit your client’s circumstances. Ensure that the advice document is structured so that you are satisfied that the client understands: the advice and recommendations you give; and the benefits of the recommended products; and the costs involved in acquiring, holding and disposing of the recommended products; and the risks involved in acquiring, holding and disposing of the products, and how you recommend they be managed. |
| **Reasonable grounds to be satisfied** | The code specifically requires that you have reasonable grounds to be satisfied that the client understands. This means documenting the conversation, questions, issues raised and following up questions and information requests. If you cannot be satisfied that your client understands your advice then you must not proceed, even if you are satisfied that it is in his/her best interest that they should proceed. |

### Standard 6: Broad Effects of Advice

You must take into account the broad effects arising from the client acting on your advice and actively consider the client’s broader, long-term interests and likely circumstances.

| **Key Components** | **Guidance** |
| --- | --- |
| **Broad effects** | You will need to specify impacts of your advice strategies, in addition to the immediate effect. For example: when recommending funding insurance from within superannuation you must document the impact that will have on long term superannuation balances; you must explain the effects on liquidity and access to funds to meet payments and costs when recommending certain assets (e.g. property in a SMSF). |
| **Long term** | Need to consider both meeting immediate and long term needs and consequences. For example: will a reasonable adviser faced with the same circumstances come to the same recommendations in order to meet long term accumulation objectives? Clients nearing retirement have greater liquidity needs than younger clients, which must be taken into account. |
| **Likely circumstances** | You must take into account the impact of the client’s circumstances. For example, an elderly or recently widowed client may not be best placed to continue to manage an existing SMSF. In these circumstances wind up of the SMSF may be in client’s best interests. |

### Standard 7: Consent, referrals and fair and reasonableness of fees

The client must give free, prior and informed consent to all benefits you and your principal will receive in connection with acting for the client, including any fees for services that may be charged. If required in the case of an existing client, the consent should be obtained as soon as practicable after this code commences.

Except where expressly permitted by the Corporations Act 2001, you may not receive any benefits, in connection with acting for a client, that derive from a third party other than your principal.

You must satisfy yourself that any fees and charges that the client must pay to you or your principal, and any benefits that you or your principal receive, in connection with acting for the client are fair and reasonable, and represent value for money for the client.

As with standard 3, this standard has been the subject of review. ASIC’s MR (19-319MR) referred to above provides that ASIC will take a *facilitative* approach to standard 7.

Financial Advice Co has taken the following approach to managing the ethical obligation of this standard.

| **Key Components** | **Guidance** |
| --- | --- |
| **Free prior and informed consent** | Refer Standard 4 – this is covered by LoEs and Ongoing Service arrangements, together with proper and detailed file notes. Consent in respect of fees received from grandfathered clients must also be received. |
| **You may not receive benefits from third party providers other than your principal.** | You, in your capacity as an Authorised Representative may not receive third party benefits from any party. |
| **Corporate Authorised Representatives may receive third party benefits** | Referral arrangements should be struck between a third-party referral source and the Corporate Authorised Representative, not the individual AR. Conflicts of interest can still arise in these circumstances and must be managed in accordance with Standard 3, above. |
| **Principal can receive third party benefits** | Your licensee can receive third party benefits as a consequence of referral arrangements. |
| **Documentation of referral arrangements** | You must keep comprehensive documents of referral arrangements in place between the CAR and third-party referrers. These documents should include due diligence on: suitability; qualifications; expertise; fee arrangements; related party interests and how conflicts are being managed. In practical terms you should have at least two (ideally three) referral options available for your client to consider. |
| **Referrals to related businesses** | When making a referral to a related business, then you must disclose to the client the nature of the relationship, any particular expertise that the related business has which makes it the best referral option, as well as establishing that the fees and charges are reasonable and represent value for money to the client. |
| **Fees and payments must represent value for money** | Fees must be set in accordance with the principles outlined in our Fees Policy. Fees may not be arbitrarily determined based on “ability to pay”, or other subjective factor. They should reflect time, effort and skill put into managing the advice. You may be asked for your reasoning regarding fees greater than $15,000 (plus GST) where we have not reviewed and approved your client pricing policy. |
