## What this policy covers

The policy provides an overview of Financial Advice Co approach to the suitability of recommending a client establish or retain a SMSF.

## What is a SMSF

*ASIC explains a SMSF: A self-managed super fund (SMSF) is a private super fund that you manage yourself. SMSFs are different to industry and retail super funds. When you manage your own super, you put the money you would normally put in a retail or industry super fund into your own SMSF. You choose the investments and the insurance. Your SMSF can have no more than six members. As a member, you are a trustee of the fund — or you can get a corporate trustee. In either case, you are responsible for the fund. While having control over your own super can be appealing, it's a lot of work and comes with risks.*

Prior to a client accepting your advice to retain or establish a SMSF it is important that they understand that they are personally liable for all the decisions made by the fund - even if the client gets help from a professional, or another member makes the decisions.

## Who is a SMSF suitable for?

A SMSF is only suitable where it is in your client’s best interest and you have satisfied yourself that:

### Clients must have sufficient financial literacy to manage superannuation matters

By definition, establishing a SMSF means there is an element of self-management. This includes not only administrative management but also investment management. Accordingly, those clients who lack financial literacy are unlikely to be suitable candidates for a SMSF.

Assessing financial literacy is to some extent subjective, but when assessing financial literacy the factors to look for include:

- Familiarity with fundamental investment principles - e.g., diversification, liquidity, volatility and investment risk/return concepts.
- Previous investment activity e.g. has the client a history of managing a portfolio of assets?
- Understanding of interest rates, inflation and time value of money.

### Clients must understand the legal, tax and administrative requirements, and the time commitment involved in managing the fund

These include establishment and ongoing management of trust deeds, the need to prepare annual accounts and lodge tax returns; additional administrative obligations when in pension phase, ensuring relevant statutory balance and transfer limits are met, and the like. These can be onerous and costly, and the client needs to understand and accept these obligations.

### Clients must understand the role of trustee

SMSF Trustees need to understand the obligations of trustee, including:

- Maintaining the fund for the sole purpose of providing retirement benefits to SMSF members, or to their dependants if a member dies before retirement.
- Accepting contributions and paying benefits (pension or lump sums) to members and their beneficiaries in accordance with superannuation and taxation laws and the SMSF trust deed.
- Valuing the fund’s assets at market value for the preparation of financial accounts and statements.
- Having the financial accounts and statements for the SMSF audited each year by an approved SMSF auditor.
- Meeting the reporting and administration obligations imposed by the Australian Taxation Office.
- Responsibility to develop, implement and review an investment strategy for the fund, which includes consideration of insurance requirements for fund members.
- Responsibility for developing an exit strategy.
- Reviewing their own ongoing suitability as Trustees of the fund.

In all circumstances, advisers should recommend that clients undertake a SMSF trustee administration course prior to deciding whether to implement advice to establish a SMSF.

The ATO publishes a list of approved SMSF trustee education courses available at [the ATO website](https://www.ato.gov.au/Super/Self-managed-super-funds/Administering-and-reporting/How-we-help-and-regulate-SMSFs/Approved-education-courses/).

### Clients must properly consider their need for appropriate insurance

Insurance within an SMSF is often more difficult to obtain and can be more expensive as automatic acceptance levels do not apply and in some instances group insurance rates are cheaper.

If you are a Limited Authorised Representative; please raise insurance with the client and that this will need to be considered. As you are not authorised to provide Insurance advice, refer the client to a Full Authorised Representative or contact us if you need to organise a referral relationship.

### Clients must understand that legal protections of SMSF are not the same as with public offer superannuation funds

Clients should be made aware, and accept, that SMSFs are not subject to the same government protections that are available in APRA-regulated superannuation funds, such as statutory compensation in the event of theft or fraud. In addition, access to dispute resolution mechanisms may be different.

### Clients must understand the costs of establishment and of ongoing management

ASIC notes (INFO 274) that there are many costs applicable to setting up, operating and winding up an SMSF that should be discussed with clients and set out in the advice. Matters that should be discussed and disclosed are:

- The costs that are expected to be incurred in establishing, operating and winding up an SMSF – in particular, which costs are unavoidable, as well as costs that may vary depending on how much of the SMSF’s administration the trustees are intending to undertake.
- How the average annual operating costs of a SMSF compare with the annual administration costs of the client’s current superannuation fund or other APRA-regulated superannuation funds.
- The cost of having professional service providers do some of the ongoing administration and management tasks for the SMSF.

### The client must understand the need for advice on continued suitability of a SMSF

This is particularly relevant:

- For existing clients who had established SMSFs with low balances when the initial advice was provided. Consideration needs to be given to ongoing appropriateness.
- Where balances fall over time (e.g. client’s in pension phase).
- Where the health or legal capacity status of the trustees changes;
- Where the residency of the members and / or trustees changes;
- Where the family circumstances and relationships between members changes; and
- Where the credit position of any of the trustees changes.

## SMSF Suitability Matrix

Your file should demonstrate how you have assessed initial and ongoing suitability of a SMSF for your clients. A SMSF Suitability Checklist has been developed to assist you with this and is available on the adviser portal. Where the client is not considered suitable you should decline to provide advice recommending establishment of a SMSF or recommend the wind up of the SMSF. You can also contact our compliance team at [contact@financialadviceco.com.au](mailto:contact@financialadviceco.com.au) for further guidance, as required.

### Examples:

| **Client circumstances** | **SMSF Suitability** |
| --- | --- |
| In accumulation phase. Current superannuation balance $250,000. Anticipating inheritance of $300,000 in next 6 months. Making regular contributions | Likely to be suitable, subject to best interests’ analysis |
| Client has a low superannuation balance. Limited ability to make contributions. Low level of financial sophistication | Unlikely to be suitable for SMSF, needs could be met by a retail fund |
| Client has large superannuation balance. In pension draw down phase | Need to monitor: As client ages and balances decline due to drawdown, SMSF may no longer be suitable. |
| Client has simple affairs (e.g. no desire to buy property or non-liquid assets) | Unlikely to be suitable – public offer or industry fund likely to be more cost effective with no administrative burden. |
| Client has substantial balance, in accumulation phase and has property or non-liquid assets | Likely to be suitable, subject to best interests’ analysis |

## ASIC Guidance

ASIC’s REP 337 notes certain red flags which suggest that an SMSF will not be suitable for an investor, including, but not limited to:

- A low fund balance where the members have a limited ability to make future contributions;
- The investor wants a simple, low-touch superannuation solution;
- The investor wants to delegate decision making to someone else;
- The investor does not have a lot of time to devote to managing their financial affairs;
- The investor has little investment decision-making experience;
- The investor, or suggested trustee, is an undischarged bankrupt or has been convicted of an offence involving dishonesty (as such, persons are prohibited from acting as a trustee); and
- The investor has a low level of financial literacy.

ASIC guidance is available at:

- INFO 274 – Tips for giving self-managed superannuation fund advice

## Advice Considerations

- **Best Interest:** Client’s best interests always apply. Even if a client requests a SMSF (in order to purchase property, say) you must still assess if that is in the client’s best interests and is in line with them achieving their goals, needs and objectives.
- **Statement of Advice:** Any advice to establish a SMSF must be documented in a Statement of Advice (SoA). Financial Advice Co does not permit establishment or wind up of a SMSF, set up of Limited Recourse Borrowing arrangements, or roll over advice on an execution only basis. Where you receive such a request you must decline the activity and explain that establishment of a SMSF has complexity and responsibility and you are only able to provide SMSF advice where you are in a position to consider the client’s personal circumstances.
- **File Evidence:** Advice to establish a SMSF must be in the client’s best interests, therefore consideration of the suitability of a SMSF is a requirement of the advice process. Your client file must be able to evidence how you determined a SMSF was suitable for the client.
- **Low balance:** In line with both ATO and ASIC guidance, Financial Advice Co position is that SMSFs may not be suitable for clients with low superannuation balances. This is consistent with ASIC’s position that SMSFs are more cost effective with larger balances. Where advice is provided to establish an SMSF with a low balance, your advice must clearly set out:
  - The circumstances that influence you to believe the client is likely to end up in a better position, despite the SMSF having a low starting balance;
  - Consideration of whether the SMSF’s intended investment strategy is appropriate and viable;
  - The reasons why setting up and operating an SMSF is in the best interests of the client.
- **Replacement Product Advice:** Establishment of a SMSF will almost always involve replacement product advice. Accordingly, where, after proper consideration, you come to the view that establishment of a SMSF is the appropriate advice for your client, taking into account their needs, goals and objectives, the relevant analysis and disclosure in relation to replacement product advice must be made in advice documents provided to your client.

Financial Advice Co Replacement Product principles apply. You must consider and include the following:

- The reasons the existing product(s) do not meet the client’s needs.
- The reasons the replacement product(s) better meet the client’s needs.
- The direct costs of obtaining the new product(s).
- The direct costs of exiting the old product(s).
- The ongoing costs of the old and new product.
- Any other consequences arising from the replacement, including where applicable, comparative costs (in dollars) and any benefits, features and options which may be lost either temporarily or permanently.

Your research of alternative product solutions must include a like-for-like comparison of the client's existing product (where applicable), and you must provide details of that comparison in the SoA so that clients can properly understand the consequences of the recommendation.

This must be described in a way so that the client can easily understand how this recommendation is in their best interests.

> **Note:** Redirection of future contributions to a SMSF constitutes a switch where there is a change in recipient fund.

You should explain to members approaching the pension phase that there may be a point at which the SMSF may cease to be cost-effective because fixed costs will remain constant or increase while the balance of the fund diminishes.

Where appropriate, you should discuss SMSF succession planning issues with members, including provisions for changing or replacing trustees and dealing with capacity issues (i.e. having suitable estate planning arrangements in place, such as Enduring Powers of Attorney):

- You must explain, for investors who are individual trustees, what will need to happen if a trustee dies or loses capacity.
- **Trustee Structure:** consideration should be given to the most appropriate trustee structure for the fund (i.e. individual or corporate) taking into account matters such as initial and ongoing cost, potential liability, asset composition, investment strategy, succession and representation issues. Where an individual trustee structure is recommended it must be clearly explained why this is favoured for the client’s circumstances over and above a corporate trustee structure which inherently carries lower risk. Financial Advice Co expectation is that a Corporate Trustee would in most situations be the preferred structure that is recommended.
