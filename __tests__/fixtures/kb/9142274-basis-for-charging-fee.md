## Background

The detail of our business is:

> **Financial Advice Co Pty Ltd**
> ABN 37 660 747 366;
> Australian Financial Services Licence Number 543 023

This policy is a subset of Financial Advice Co Pty Ltd compliance framework, designed to ensure that Financial Advice Co and its Authorised Representatives meets its obligations under the law and act in manner consistent with the provision of quality advice to its clients, and applies to those Authorised Representatives who are authorised by Financial Advice Co to provide full advice to retail clients.

## To whom does this policy apply

This policy applies to those Advisers whom Financial Advice Co have authorised to provide Advice under its AFSL and clients fall under retail advice.

## Discretion

We draft our policies to reflect Financial Advice Co understanding of the law and our position on managing risk. However, we also understand that in some cases client circumstances may justify a variation to our policy position. If, based on your client circumstances, you believe that a variation to our policy in respect of a particular client is justified then please discuss with your compliance contact, who will consider the circumstances and advise you if a variation to policy is warranted.

Please note that until such time as Financial Advice Co provides you with written approval to your request, the standard policy requirements as documented in this policy apply.

Please submit your request for policy variation to [contact@financialadviceco.com.au](mailto:contact@financialadviceco.com.au)

## General obligations

a. Ensure that when charging a fee, the client understands:

i. The cost of the services offered before they commit to paying the fees;
ii. How much the fees are and when they are payable;
iii. Any conflicts of interest that may arise.

b. The FSG outlines the general basis and parameters for remuneration, including advice fees. You must disclose all specific remuneration attributable to personal advice in the relevant client advice document, SOA or ROA, inclusive of GST (all parts and any supplementary where applicable). The disclosures are to be in dollar terms where possible.

c. Remuneration includes payments received by:

i. the licensee;
ii. the practice;
iii. the employer of the Authorised Representative (if this is different from the practice);
iv. the Authorised Representative; and
v. any associate of any of the above (which includes any related bodies corporate or trusts under which any of the above is a beneficiary).

d. Fees can be charged for:

i. providing advice - initial and ongoing;
ii. implementation of advice; and
iii. ongoing services

e. Other remuneration includes

i.referral fees/commissions paid to and received from third parties (including shared commission arrangements). Refer to the Referral Arrangements Policy for full requirements relating to referral fees/commissions;
ii. commissions/dealer margins/platform bonuses/rebates. Refer to the Prohibited Benefits section of the Your Responsibilities Policy for further information regarding commissions;
iii. any other fees, commissions or other remuneration that relates to the advice not identified above.

f. Implement and maintain a practice-based pricing policy which outlines the fees applicable to the initial and ongoing advice services that your practice offers.

## Dating of documents

Note that fee and renewal agreements must reflect the actual date the client signs the documents. The date must never be pre-filled, back dated, or post-dated.

## Fee Methodology

Fees, including ongoing fees may be charged on the following basis, with the overriding obligation that you must act honestly, fairly and efficiently.

i. An hourly rate;
ii. Event based, for SOA, implementation;
iii. A percentage of Assets;
iv. Agreed flat fee.

## Ongoing advice service for grandfathered products

Where you are providing ongoing advice to a client who holds a product that allows a grandfathered payment:

a. Determine the fair value of providing advice to the client based on your pricing policy.

b. Determine the current break up of this cost (e.g. adviser service fee, grandfathered trail commissions, grandfathered volume bonuses, etc.) and separate into direct (i.e. adviser service fee) and indirect (e.g. grandfathered trail commission) costs.

## What services can be charged for?

Sect. 962A of the Corporations act states that if, under the terms of the arrangement, a fee (however described or structured) is to be paid during a period of more than 12 months, the arrangement is an ongoing fee arrangement. Accordingly:

a. Any personal advice can be charged for. Services such as portfolio reviews, advice on rebalancing, advice on changed financial circumstances, and the like are appropriate to be covered in a client review and charged for. Such personal advice must be documented in a RoA, or SoA, as appropriate.

b. Ongoing fees may be charged for other services, including but not limited to provision of information to your client, or assistance in acting as a conduit between the client and other professional advisers, provided the services for which the fees are being charged are properly documented, explained and agreed to by the client.

c. Note that services such as client newsletters, invitations to functions or briefings, whilst valuable to the ongoing client relationship, will only partially satisfy your obligations to deliver service under an ongoing service arrangement.

d. Any advice or service that can be directly attributable to the ongoing service or advice of the client can be charged for. (See ongoing service arrangements).

## Fees for no service

a. Failure to deliver services that have been agreed with the client and charged for is a serious matter, and constitute Fees for No Service, and will be reported to the Risk & Compliance Committee for further action, which may include reporting to ASIC.

b. Failure to deliver services occurs when an ongoing advice fee has been charged with an expectation of a review to the client and no review has been provided within 12 months of the previous advice.

c. Where despite your best efforts a client does not participate in a review service previously agreed you must provide in writing, a review of the strategy and portfolio as per the previous advice, including any rebalancing recommendations. Failure to provide a review on at least an annual basis may result in fees being refunded. In this case, you should seriously consider terminating the ongoing fee arrangement.

d. You must reimburse clients for any review service paid for, but not delivered. The reimbursement should include an interest component to compensate clients for the time value of money lost. The appropriate rate of interest should be equivalent to the actual investment return that a client might have received in the portfolio upon which advice has been provided. ASIC RG 256 notes that where it is not possible or reasonably practicable to determine the actual investment returns or interest that a client would have received, it is appropriate to use the cash rate set by the Reserve Bank of Australia plus 6%.

e. When providing refunds, you must ensure that it does not cause a client further financial loss or detriment – for example, by breaching a contribution cap for a superannuation account.

f. If you have charged your client fees in a bundled manner, you must have an evidence-based methodology for apportioning value to each service. You must also prominently disclose this apportionment to each client.

g. If a fee arrangement is terminated, and your client subsequently reconnects for ongoing service, a new fee arrangement and service agreement should be reached and documented in an advice document.

## Apportionment of fees

When providing advice to individuals as well as corporate or trust structures (e.g. personal advice to the beneficiaries of an SMSF as well as to the SMSF), or to more than one individual in a family situation, then fees must be properly apportioned to each person or entity receiving advice. Apportionmentshould be:

a. Based on your pricing policy;
b. Reflective of the advice services provided to each entity (bearing in mind the sole purpose test obligations associated with superannuation).

## You must never

a. Charge an asset-based fee on any part of a geared portfolio for a new or existing client. This includes initial and ongoing fees. The ban applies to all borrowed money, but does not apply to internally geared products.

b. Charge more than the lesser of $4,000 (GST inclusive) or the value of 2 hours of your time for an Execution Only transaction, irrespective of whether the payment is received as a fee or commission.

c. Receive a fee directly from a client.
