## What is a Record of Advice?

A record of advice (ROA) is a record of any further advice provided to an existing client after the original Statement of Advice (SOA). A Record of Advice is a regulated document designed to facilitate the streamlined provision of incremental, consistent and subsequent advice. It is often given to existing clients to confirm changes to, or implementation of, advice that has been provided in a previous SOA.

## When can I use a Record of Advice?

### Further Advice

You can use an RoA to give further advice to the client, where all of the below are satisfied:

- you have previously given the client an SOA setting out the client’s relevant circumstances in relation to the advice (the ‘previous advice’);
- the client’s relevant circumstances in relation to the further advice are not significantly different from when the client received the previous advice; and
- to the extent the further advice is based on matters other than the relevant circumstances of the client – the basis on which the further advice is given is not significantly different from the basis on which the previous advice was given. (see regulation 7.7.10AE)

You must therefore conduct reasonable inquiries that confirm that the client’s relevant circumstances have not changed significantly and record this information and your assessment appropriately as per the Data Collection & Risk Profiling Policy requirements.

You can also use an RoA to provide advice in limited situations where an SoA has not been previously provided:

### No buy or sell product advice

You give advice that does not recommend, or state an opinion about, the acquisition or disposal of a specific financial product, and does not recommend a modification to an investment strategy or a contribution level for a financial product held by the client, and:

- you and certain associates do not receive any remuneration or benefit in relation to the advice, and
- you provide information about potential conflicts in your advice to the client: see section 946B(7)–(8).

Typically you could rely on this exemption from providing an SOA if you are giving an initial free appointment or if you receive no remuneration for giving your clients ‘no action or hold’ type advice. Advisers with ongoing service arrangements will not satisfy the requirements to use an ROA under this exemption, where an SoA has not been previously provided, because they are receiving remuneration in relation to the advice.

### Small Investment Advice

You give advice that relates to financial investments in certain products not exceeding $15,000: see section 946AA and reg 7.7.09A. The $15,000 limit is for **the total value of all financial investments the advice relates to**. Please note that this does not apply to advice in relation to superannuation products unless the client already holds an interest in that product. Small Investment RoAs must be given to the client.

## What does a significant change to relevant circumstances mean?

There are no hard and fast rules in legislation therefore the ordinary meaning of the word should be applied, e.g. meaningful, major, material, fairly large in amount or quantity. You should regularly update client fact finds and risk profile analysis to confirm if there are material changes to the client’s circumstances, including financial situation, changes in personal circumstances and changes in goals, needs and objectives.

It is ultimately up to you to determine and record in your file if a change in your view is not significant enough to warrant the use of an SOA. If you believe relevant changes may be significant, take a conservative view and address the issues in a new SOA and incorporate by reference information from the previous SOA.

The following are some examples from ASIC INFO 266 of significant changes that may mean an ROA is not suitable.

- a new mortgage or significant increase or decrease in debt
- divorce or separation from a partner
- a new baby
- significant home renovations
- redundancy or job loss
- a significant increase or decrease in income
- inheritance
- sale of business
- significant health events (i.e. terminal illness, disability or trauma)
- retirement
- death of a partner, and
- a move to aged care.

In addition,

- Changes to the client’s risk profile may be significant and require an SOA.
- Small changes of a client’s circumstances that accumulate over time, for example, several pay increases that eventually result in a significant change since the original SOA.

## What does change in basis of advice mean?

The following are some examples of significant changes to the basis of advice, meaning an ROA is not suitable.

- New type of product class (advice was insurance, now is super).
- Adding a gearing strategy.
- Starting a pension (when previously accumulating).
- Changing or adding product types (e.g. managed fund to MDA, ETF’s to Direct Shares or super).
- New legislation may create or remove the ability to recommend a current or new strategy.
- Changes to the broader environment or economy may be significant and require an SOA (e.g. Covid).

Switching underlying funds within a client’s existing platform to continue the same strategy would not be considered a change in the basis of advice. Likewise recommending a different product in the same class as previously recommended does not constitute a change in the basis of advice. For example, changing from one platform to another, or one income protection policy to another, where the underlying strategy is the same and there are no significant changes to the client’s relevant circumstances driving the product change.

These are guidelines, but generally your strategies must be relatively consistent with the previous SOA. If you believe the strategy you are advising on now to be significantly different, you must provide your advice in a new SOA, which could incorporate by reference information from the previous SOA.

## Contents of a ROA

While the legislation does not mandate a particular format, our requirement is that a Record of Advice must be in writing. The Record of Advice must be retained for a minimum of 7 years and made available to the client if and when requested.

The following must be included in your Record of Advice:

- confirming the date of the previous SOA that this advice is further to;
- a clear statement that information about the client’s relevant circumstances is set out in a previous SOA provided to the client;
- confirmation that the client’s relevant circumstances are not significantly different to those in the previous SoA;
- confirmation that the basis on which the further advice is given is not significantly different from the basis on which the previous advice was given;
- the recommendations (advice) made to the client, including the basis on which the recommendations were made;
- information disclosed to the client under s947D(2) – Replacement Product costs;
- information that would be required in an SOA about potential conflicts of interest under s947B(2);
- any relevant warnings.

When using templates and preparing ROAs, you should consider presenting the information in a way that is easy for the client to access, digest and understand.

## When must a ROA be prepared?

If you are preparing a ROA after the advice has been communicated to the client, for example after a review meeting or phone call, you should create the record immediately or as soon as practicable. For example, within 2 business days.

## Documenting your ROA

- Your Records of Advice must be recorded using an approved Licensee template.
- Our policy is to have a written ROA on your file as it is easier to reference that further advice has been provided. It also ensures that you meet your legal obligations and if the client does request a copy of the record of further advice, it can easily be produced.
- You must receive your client’s consent to proceed with the recommendations contained in your ROA. Accordingly, this is easier to demonstrate where the advice is documented for your client. Best practice is for the client to sign the ROA or for you to have an email confirmation from the client consenting to proceed with the advice. A contemporaneous file note that demonstrates the client has accepted the advice (e.g. in the advice meeting or on a phone call) is also acceptable.
- Where your further advice results in no changes then a Record of Advice reflecting that outcome is required. This must be available to the client upon request and must also be recorded as the result of that review with your client.

| --- |
| The title “Record of Advice” must be used |
| How the further advice was provided – Verbally (Phone), Written (Email, Document) |
| Who the advice was provided to |
| Date of the Statement of Advice this is further advice to |
| How the advice was initiated i.e., Client or Adviser |
| Document any relevant changes to the client’s circumstances and an explanation as to why you believe they are not significant. |
| Scope of the advice and exclusions |
| Information about the basis on which the advice is or was given. |
| An explanation of the reasons for the recommendations including a generic description of the range of products or classes of products or strategies considered and investigated, the strategy selected, the asset allocation, the product provider and particular product. The explanations must include advantages and disadvantages of the recommendations; |
| The explanations must also include how it satisfies the best interest of the client, i.e. how it leaves the client in a better position; |
| If you recommend that a client replace an existing financial product with another financial product you must comply by including the following requirements: any charges the client will or may incur in respect of the disposal or reduction; any charges the client will or may incur in respect of the acquisition or increase; any pecuniary or other benefits that the client will or may lose (temporarily or otherwise) as a result of taking the recommended action (i.e. benefits lost); information about any other significant consequences for the client of taking the recommended action that the providing entity knows, or ought reasonably to know, are likely; the basis for the recommendation. |
| Disclosure of any remuneration, other benefits and or any conflicts; Fees for existing products that have previously been disclosed in an SOA do not need to be redisclosed in the ROA.
