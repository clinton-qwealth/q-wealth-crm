## Definition of best interest

The best interest’s duty is a legislative requirement to ensure the processes and motivations of Authorised Representatives are focused on what is best for their clients. In addition, the Adviser must adhere to the Code of Ethics. Standard 2 of the Code of Ethics requires Advisers to act with integrity and in the best interest of each of your clients. Refer to the Code of Ethics Guidance for more information.

First and foremost, it is about providing advice that is likely to leave the client in a better position. This means prioritising your clients interests above yours, and providing advice that meets the client’s goals, needs and expectations after taking into account their circumstances.

The test that is applied is whether a reasonable advice provider would believe that the client is likely to be in a better position if the client follows the advice. Importantly, advice does not have to be “perfect”.

## Advice obligations

Ensure your advice process and recommendations capture the following:

- A clearly defined scope that is appropriate to the subject matter of advice sought by the client and the client’s relevant circumstances.
- An investigation of the client’s relevant circumstances.
- Assistance given by you to the client, to set and prioritise specific and measurable goals and objectives.
- Where relevant, consideration of potential strategies and options that are available to the client to meet their objectives and needs.
- Consideration of relevant aspects of the impact of the advice - for example, tax or social security consequences.
- Good communication with the client. This includes:
  - Providing advice documents that are logically structured and easy to understand; and
  - Verbal interactions that aim to ensure that the advice and recommendations are understood.
- Strategic and product recommendations that are appropriate for the client’s relevant circumstances.
- Any other factors you have considered in reaching your recommendations with associated documentation in either a file note or advice document.
- File notes to record all conversations.

## Complying with best interests

You will have satisfied the best interest duty if you are able to show that:

- You have identified the objectives, financial situation and needs of the client
- Identified
  - the subject matter of the advice that has been sought by the client (whether explicitly or implicitly); and
  - the objectives, financial situation and needs of the client that would reasonably be considered as relevant to advice sought
- Made reasonable inquiries to obtain complete and accurate information;
- Assessed whether you have the expertise required to provide the client advice on the subject matter sought and, if not, declined to provide the advice;
- If where you have come to the view that it is reasonable to recommend a financial product that you have:
  - conducted a reasonable investigation into the financial products that might achieve the objectives and needs of the client that would reasonably be considered as relevant to the advice; and
  - assessed the information gathered in the investigation;
- Based all judgements in advising the client on the client’s relevant circumstances;
- Taken any other step that, at the time the advice is provided, would reasonably be regarded as being in the best interests of the client, given the client’s relevant circumstances.

Documenting how you have applied these requirements is essential for evidencing that you have met your obligations. This will include using, for example, data collection documents, file notes, advice documents, product replacement checklists, etc.

## The seven steps

To comply with your duty to act in the best interests of your client, you will need to prove that you have followed seven requirements (also referred to as steps) below. Collectively these seven steps are referred to as “the Safe Harbour”.

If you can prove that you have taken the steps outlined below, you can show that you have complied with the best interest duty. You are required to comply with all seven steps and all the other related obligations. If you cannot meet the following steps, you should not proceed with the advice as you will not be able to satisfy the best interest requirement.

Please note, these steps are not linear and are applied at different stages throughout the advice process.

## Acting on client’s instructions

In some circumstances a client may request specific outcomes e.g. to create an SMSF, or to move funds into a specific asset allocation, or to invest in asset classes contrary to their risk profile.

Your obligation to provide advice in the client’s best interest, even in these circumstances, remain. Your role is not to be an order taker – your role is to provide advice, and it is not appropriate to provide execution only advice in these circumstances. If in doubt consult Financial Advice Co Compliance at [contact@financialadviceco.com.au](mailto:contact@financialadviceco.com.au)

Therefore, any advice that flows will be personal advice, which means the best interest obligation must be met. In these circumstances, you must:

- Perform your usual analysis to determine what solution is in the client’s best interest;
- Document your analysis and advice in an advice document;
- Inform client in writing of any cost, benefit, risks or consequences upon their objectives, needs and priorities; or upon previously implemented recommendations that flow from implementing their direction;
- If the client’s direction is not in their best interest, then you must obtain explicit consent to implement contrary to advice, or consider declining to implement. Your warnings must be in writing and signed off by the client.

## You must not

- Provide personal advice to a client where you cannot demonstrate that you have complied with best interest’s duty and how the client is likely to be in a better position as a result of your advice.
- Engage in churning (i.e. the deliberate reinvestment of a client’s funds or re-writing of insurance without a demonstrable, tangible benefit to the client), designed to deliver a financial benefit to the Authorised Representative. A minor benefit is not considered a good enough reason to facilitate a change. As a general rule a saving of less than $250 is considered minor, absent other compelling reasons to change.

## Recording that you have acted in the best interest of the client

You must maintain robust record keeping to demonstrate compliance. Most steps will be captured in the advice documents such as the SOA; however, you must maintain records that provide coverage of client’s interest and how your actions are likely to leave them in a better position.

The most effective proof or evidence is documents undertaken at the time the advice was given, e.g. advice documents, file notes, working papers and fact-find documents. These documents will help demonstrate that you have satisfied your best interest duty.
