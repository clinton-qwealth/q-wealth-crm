## What is replacement product advice?

- Replacement product advice is defined as any recommendation to dispose or reduce the level of an existing or future interest in a financial product, and instead acquire or increase the level of interest in another financial product.
- You cannot disclaim responsibility for replacement product advice. For example, you cannot state in your advice document that the advice *“does not include advice about the ‘from’ fund”*. Such disclaimers are of no value and cannot be included. If you have insufficient information to make a properly informed recommendation you should decline to provide the advice.

## Hold / Retain Recommendations

- You should periodically consider, based on your professional judgement whether there is a need to scope in advice on the existing product (e.g., is the platform or only the underlying portfolio in scope?). In any case you must explain the reasons for the scoping. (e.g., the client does not want advice on the platform, or you and the client agree that recent advice provided on the platform does not require new advice)
- A hold recommendation is a recommendation even though you are not replacing; therefore, you are required to keep some level of research completed on the existing product and possible alternatives in your file to evidence it remains appropriate.
- A hold or retain recommendation does not require a product replacement table or like for like comparison in your SoA but you should include that you have considered alternatives in coming to the conclusion that retaining the existing product is appropriate.
- You do not need to have the TMD on file for hold recommendations unless new money is invested.

## Advice documentation

When product replacement advice is being provided, the advice document must include sufficient explanation of:

- Why the replacement product has been recommended; and
- How the use of this new product will put the client in a better position compared to retaining an existing product.

This Replacement Product information must be set out in a table and include the following:

- The reasons the existing product(s) do not meet the client’s needs.
- The reasons the replacement product(s) better meets the client’s needs.
- The direct costs of obtaining the new product(s).
- The direct costs of exiting the old product(s).
- You should also compare the ongoing costs of the old and new product.
- Any other significant consequences arising from the replacement, including where applicable, comparative costs (in dollars) and any benefits, features and options which may be lost either temporarily or permanently and any CGT consequences.

## Alternatives

- Any alternative product must be a viable option that could meet the client’s needs and objectives. When comparing a recommended product to an alternative product, your starting point for a viable alternative is the APL. If you are unable to find a viable option on the APL you must consider alternative products outside the APL. A viable alternative product must be the same or similar in type and features and be able to satisfy the relevant needs of the client.
- When replacing an existing product, at least two relevant alternatives need to be considered and compared against the recommended product, one of which can be the client’s existing product.

## Like for Like Comparison

The purpose of a Like for Like comparison is to demonstrate that you have investigated alternative products, including the existing product on equivalent terms or as close to equal as possible.

This would include aligning things such as asset allocation, investment style, benefit amounts, premium types etc.

For example, when switching to a new product with a growth asset allocation from a product with a balanced asset allocation, or an active fund to an index fund you must demonstrate you have compared a growth asset allocation or an index option in the existing fund.

Your file must include research of alternative product solutions on a like-for-like basis. You must also provide details of that comparison in the alternative section of the SoA. The information may also be presented in table format in the advice document.

This must be described in a way that the client can easily understand how this recommendation is in their best interests.

## Other considerations

- In circumstances where advice relates only to redirection of future contributions you must still explain the basis for that recommendation. This requires explaining and disclosure of costs, benefits and significant consequences of redirecting those contributions, including advantages and disadvantages.
- Note that ASIC takes the position that Super Switching advice will generally be inappropriate if the Authorised Representative knew (or should have known) that:
  - The overall benefits likely to result from the ‘to’ fund would be lower than under the ‘from’ fund, unless outweighed by overall cost savings. (As a general rule cost savings should not be immaterial).
  - The cost of the ‘to’ fund is higher than the ‘from’ fund, unless the ‘to’ fund better satisfies your client’s needs.
- It is not appropriate to state there is a benefit within the ‘to’ product or fund if the benefit is already available in their ‘from’ product or fund.
- Please refer to the SMSF Suitability policy for further details on providing SMSF advice.

## Insurance switches

- If you are recommending that the client switch insurance products, or superannuation products that include insurance, you must ensure new cover is in place before the old cover is terminated. The advice to retain cover pending new cover being in force must be evidenced by a file note of the discussion held, as well as being detailed in the SOA. You must not change a policy unless there is sufficient advantage for the client to do so.

Special care must be taken to ensure that your client understands and accepts any waiting period or exclusions for pre-existing conditions that may be imposed by the new insurer. In these cases, such conditions are likely to preclude you from making the recommendation as the loss of benefit might be substantial.

You must also confirm with your client that there has been no change in their circumstances in the period between applying for the new insurance cover and acceptance. If there have been any changes and these have not been disclosed to the new insurer then your client’s coverage may be at risk.

- Switching insurance, where there is no material benefit to your client is not permitted. This practice, known as churning, is not tolerated.
