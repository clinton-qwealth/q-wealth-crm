## Purpose of Fact-Finding

Advice is typically provided to a client as part of a process that includes one or more consultations with the client to make inquiries about the client’s relevant circumstances.

You are required to investigate, obtain and record adequate information about a client’s circumstances, including objectives, (including investment objectives when relevant) financial situation and particular needs before making any recommendations to a client.

Fact finding consultations must have a genuine purpose of identifying the subject matter of advice sought by the client or of making inquiries about the client’s relevant circumstances.

The client must be clearly informed at the outset of each fact-finding consultation that:

- the purpose is to discover the client’s relevant circumstances so that personal advice can be provided;
- the personal advice will be set out or recorded in an SOA or a ROA; and
- the client should not act on any representations made during the consultations. You should make file notes of client discussions supporting your fact-finding process.

## Client Goals

- You should capture the clients' goals in the fact find document as stated in their own words. You may clarify these goals by asking further questions to assist in the provision of your advice and also record these in the fact find document.
- You should give assistance to the client, if required, to obtain prioritised, specific and measurable goals and objectives.

## Scope of Advice

- The scope of advice must be recorded in the fact find and agreed with the client.
- You can adjust the level of your inquiries to reflect the nature of the advice sought, however you still must investigate whether there are any critical issues or broader circumstances that are relevant to the scope of your advice.
- You must consider whether the information initially disclosed for the fact find is sufficient to identify the client’s relevant circumstances. You may need to make further inquiries to identify the client’s relevant circumstances.
- Where you believe a different or additional area of advice should be included or the scope is beyond the client’s affordability, you should discuss this with the client and the client may decide to revise the scope of the advice to more suitable areas you identified.
- You must clarify and confirm the scope of the advice sought, especially when you have identified changes. You would need to renegotiate the scope, obtain client agreement and record this process before proceeding further.
- You can amend the scope of the advice with the client’s prior agreement, however you cannot exclude critical issues that are relevant to areas of the advice sought.
- Any relevant areas of advice that are scoped out must be explained and the reason why it was scoped out needs to be recorded in the fact find and the advice document. You should warn the client if there are any issues if the area was not scoped in. You should carefully consider whether you can proceed with advice if critical areas are not in the scope.
- You must consider and record your investigation of the client’s financial literacy and understanding of relevant financial topics. This may require you to make additional inquiries where the advice is relatively complex.

## Fact Find Documentation

- The fact find document that you use must be the approved licensee template unless you have specific permission to use an alternative version.
- Complete all relevant sections of your fact-finding documents. Where areas are not completed you must identify why it has not been completed, which may be because it is “Not Applicable”, “Not in Scope”, or the “Client has Declined” or the information is provided in a separate document. If the information is approximate only, a note is to be made to this effect. If the client declines to provide the information, a note to this effect is to be recorded for the question involved. You should consider whether you can proceed with providing advice that must include a tailored warning that the information is incomplete or inaccurate.
- Where your approved financial planning software contains a Fact Find or reverse fact find, you may use this instead of the licensee template with the same obligations as per this policy.
- In all cases, ensure that the client has confirmed that the information you have collected is accurate. Best practice is for the fact find to be signed by the client. Digital signatures are permitted. An email confirmation or other video recording of the client confirmation is also acceptable. In limited circumstances, for example, where you are unable to obtain a signature or recording due to client circumstances, you may file note the client confirmation which should include the reason why a signature or recording was not available.
- For existing clients, you must make additional inquiries about the client circumstances if applicable and whether the information already held is up-to-date and complete. You do not need the client to resupply information you already hold or complete a new fact find each review. For example, for further advice to an existing client you may use a reverse fact find or capture the changes in a file note.
- The fact find template includes client declarations and acknowledgements, therefore if you are not using the standard Licensee fact find template, you still must ensure the clients sign the relevant declarations and acknowledgments.
- Each client’s information must be stored securely together in one electronic file. Regulators take the view that the file stands on its own – in other words if it’s not in the file, it doesn’t exist. This means that it will be unacceptable if called upon to provide a client file for review by Financial Advice Co or anyone else, if you have to go to multiple information sources to obtain the full picture.
- You may only collect client information from a third party where you have the client’s explicit written consent to do so. Equally you or your staff cannot hold out that you are another person to whom consent may have been given.
- At no time should clients be asked to sign incomplete or blank forms.

## Rules for specific fact-finding circumstances

These rules expand on fact finding inquires to evidence your investigation into the clients’ relevant circumstances. We expect you may need to make additional inquiries. Also, refer to the specific policies where applicable.

### Gearing

- You must investigate and discuss the clients cashflow position.
- You must collect information on the clients existing insurance arrangements and complete a full fact find (as fully as practicable with relevance to gearing).
- Take a conservative, worst case view of a client's gross income - particularly if a client's income fluctuates from year to year.
- Assess the emotional ability of the client to cope with leveraged gains and losses as a result of market movements.

### Superannuation

- You must have a copy of current existing member statements on file, including their beneficiary nominations and any insurance details.
- You must obtain details of their contribution history, particularly if you are advising on contributions.

### SMSF

- For existing SMSFs, you must obtain, review and have the SMSF trust deed and Investment strategy on file.
- You must complete and annotate the SMSF suitability checklist for new clients with a SMSF, SMSF establishment as well as existing SMSF clients where appropriate to do so.

### Insurance

- It is important to discuss affordability in addition to needs.
- You must investigate the client’s attitude to potential trade-offs of using super for insurance premiums.
- Inquire into the clients understanding of different insurance product types.
- If the client has directed a specific insurance need (such as a dollar amount of Life insurance) you must make inquiries to identify the client’s basis for the amount specified.
- You must inquire if the client has existing cover and their claims and health history.

### Environmental, Social and Governance Preferences

- You must inquire about the clients' preferences in regard to investing with Environmental, Social and Governance considerations.
- You must evidence further discussion of your enquiries via the fact find or additional file notes.

### Investments

Consider questions and answers on the following issues when discussing investment goals, objectives and preference with clients:

- existing approach to investments
- financial investment experience and examples
- need for regular income (e.g. retirement income)
- need for capital growth
- desire to minimise fees and costs
- preference for lower volatility investments
- tolerance for the risk of capital loss, especially where this is a significant possibility if the advice is followed
- tolerance for the risk that the advice (if followed) will not produce the expected benefits. For example, in the context of retirement advice, this may include considering longevity risk, market risk and inflation risk, existing investment portfolio
- existing debts
- investment horizon
- need to be able to readily ‘cash in’ the investment
- capacity to service any loan used to acquire a financial product, including the client’s ability to respond to any margin call or make good any losses sustained while investing in leveraged products; and
- tax position, social security entitlements, family commitments, employment security and expected retirement age.

## Risk profiling

When providing investment advice (including superannuation), you must establish the client’s risk tolerance, capacity and understanding of financial risks.

The Client Risk Profiling Tool (available on the portal) helps you to determine your client’s attitude to risk and volatility. Understanding this helps you prepare advice appropriate for your client. The following guidance is provided:

- Complete the Risk Profiler with the client. You may forward it to the client ahead of your meeting as long as you hold discussions with them about it and take account of any notes they have made in it prior to them signing or confirming it. If you wish to use an alternate risk profiling tool you must seek Financial Advice Co’s consent and approval for doing so.
- Risk profiles should be revisited with your client at each review. You should complete a new risk profile if required, for example, if the client does not agree with the existing assessment or there have been significant changes in client circumstances, such as a move to retirement. Updated risk profiles must be confirmed by your client/s.
- Ensure you have accurately risk profiled your clients, based on the information they have provided to you as it is your obligation to ensure that the risk profile and asset allocation that you recommend are appropriate for their goals, objectives and risk tolerance levels.
- You MUST get the client(s) to confirm the agreed risk profile and asset allocation before using it as the basis of advice by either having the client sign the risk profile document or confirming the outcome in another way in writing, such as email.
- Use the Strategic Asset Allocations that align to the clients the Risk Profile as provided by the Licensee. If you vary the client risk profile, the variation must be driven by the client’s circumstances and the reasons for the variation documented in the Risk Profiler or your file notes, together with the risks which arise as a result of that deviation within the advice document.

## Clients with special needs

A client with special needs is a client that has difficulty understanding or comprehending financial concepts. This may be due to health, age, mental capacity, education, the influence of drugs/alcohol, financial/general literacy, or other circumstances.

Where you have identified a client has a special need, you must obtain an appropriate person to assist e.g., a guardian, power of attorney or suitable family member (provided under POA) nominated by the client. The third party cannot be an employee of your practice or the licensee.

You must be satisfied the nominated third party has a clear understanding of what is occurring and is assisting the client to understand the advice.

- Exercise extreme care when dealing with a client with an intellectual disability. You must deal with a power of attorney or legally appointed entity that is authorised to make financial decisions. Ideally you will also be dealing with their guardian, if applicable.
- Ensure you make comprehensive file notes regarding the process followed and sequence of events where a third party is involved in the advice process e.g., a POA, guardian, etc. This could include, for example, identifying the need for a third party to become involved and recording details of the third party.
- If relying on a Power of Attorney, you must sight a certified copy and retain a copy on file.
- Ensure there is a valid POA in place at the time advice is to be provided, which grants the attorney authority in relation to that advice.
- Adhere to AML identification requirements for both the POA and the client.

Any client with special needs that fall outside of this policy, please contact Financial Advice Co at [contact@financialadviceco.com.au](mailto:contact@financialadviceco.com.au) for assistance.

### Non-English-Speaking Clients

Note that where you provide advice to clients who do not speak English, unless you are fluent in their first language, you must deal through a properly authorised Power of Attorney, acting on the client’s behalf (in English). All documentation, advice documents, file notes and the like are created and stored in English. Advice documents must be provided in English and if needed must be translated at your expense, by a certified translator for the client and stored in your files. **You must be able to evidence that you have reasonable grounds to be satisfied the client has understood your questions and the information you have provided.**

## Privacy

- The client must be provided with the Privacy Collection Statement, before, or at the time you collect personal information.
- Explain Financial Advice Co Privacy Policy prior to collecting personal information.
- Obtain client consent to collect, use, disclose and maintain their personal information for the purpose of provision of advice. If the client declines, you cannot proceed with the interview any further and must destroy the information provided. Privacy policy should be confirmed with the client prior to data collection.
- Only collect information reasonably needed for the provision of advice.
- Tax File numbers, credit card and bank account details must only be used for the purpose provided. If recorded in a client file they must be securely stored with password protection.
- Destroy, de-identify or return unsolicited client information as soon as practicable where you determine it is not required to provide financial services to the client.
- Have the client complete and sign the relevant permission forms where you need to collect information about the client from a third party (e.g., a superannuation provider).
- Only disclose client information to a third party (including a related business) where the law permits and in accordance with Financial Advice Co privacy policy; this would typically be where the client gives consent, or it is required for legal or enforcement reasons.
- Take reasonable steps to ensure the recipient won’t breach the Australian Privacy Principles before disclosing client information to overseas recipients.
- If you use external paraplanners or support services that are located overseas you must disclose this to your client.
