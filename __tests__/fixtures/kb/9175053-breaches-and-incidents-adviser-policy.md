## Background

The details of our business is:

> **Financial Advice Co Pty Ltd**
> ABN 37 660 747 366;
> Australian Financial Services Licence Number 543 023

This policy is a subset of Financial Advice Co compliance framework, designed to ensure that Financial Advice Co and its Authorised Representatives meets their obligations under the law and act in a manner consistent with the provision of quality advice to its clients.

## To whom does this policy apply

This Policy applies to all Representatives (including directors, authorised representatives, and financial advisers) whom Financial Advice Co have authorised to provide advice under its license.

## Purpose

The objectives of this Policy are to provide direction for Authorised Representatives and Financial Advice Co with regards to:

- identification of breaches or likely breaches
- determining whether identified breaches and incidents, or likely breaches and incidents, are significant
- when to report to ASIC and other regulators that those breaches or likely breaches are significant
- how to identify and address when the notify, investigate, and remediate obligations apply.

## Overview of breach reporting framework

The framework for the reporting and management of reportable situations is shown below and covered in detail in this policy.

### Key terminology

- **Breach** - A breach is a contravention or likely contravention of a regulatory or legislative obligation. Upon investigation, a breach may be categorised as a ‘Reportable Situation’.
- **Reportable situation** - A significant breach or likely significant breach of core obligations contained in s912A of the Corporations law, or a deemed significant breach under the legislation (refer Appendix 1 for description of what a deemed reportable breach is).

Please refer to the Glossary at the end of this document for further explanation on Key terminologies used in this Guide.

## Incidents and Breaches

### What is an incident

The use of the term ‘incident’ refers to non-compliance with Financial Advice Co’s policies, standards and other business requirements. It includes matters which are not a breach but are a recommendation to improve a process, advice document or record keeping as a matter of best practice.

### What is a breach

A breach is a contravention or likely contravention of a regulatory or legislative obligation.

This means that an incident may become a breach depending on the nature of the incident, the frequency of the incident and the impact the incident may have on Financial Advice Co’s ability to monitor and supervise the activities of its advisers, and the detriment, if any to clients.

### What are non reportable breaches?

If an incident meets the criteria above it is unlikely to be a reportable breach, although the incident must be considered in its context.

Additionally, the legislation provides that certain breaches are exempted from the automatically reportable breach regime. This includes all the FDS related civil penalty provisions other than charging a fee after an arrangement has terminated (Section 962P) and arranging for deduction of ongoing fees without consent (Section 962R).

Note that even though these are excluded from automatic reporting they still need to be investigated to determine if in fact they are significant and should be reported.

Refer to Appendix 1 for a listing of sections exempted from the automatic reporting obligations.

## What situations must be reported to ASIC?

Financial Advice Co must report to ASIC **within 30 days** after Financial Advice Co first **knows**, or should have reasonably known, that a reportable situation has arisen. **Reportable situations** include:

- Reportable situations about **core obligations:**
  - Financial Advice Co or a representative of Financial Advice Co breach a core obligation **and** the breach is **significant.**
  - Financial Advice Co or a representative of Financial Advice Co is no longer able to comply with a **core obligation** and the breach will be **significant** if it occurs (‘**likely breach’**).
  - Financial Advice Co investigates whether there has been a breach, or likely will be a breach, of a core obligation, and the investigation continues for more than 30 days.
  - Financial Advice Co must report to ASIC on the outcome of the investigation referred to above, once it is complete (including where no breach of core obligations has occurred).
- **Additional reportable situations** (must be reported irrespective of whether it is ‘significant’):
  - Financial Advice Co or a representative of Financial Advice Co has engaged in conduct constituting **gross negligence.**
  - Financial Advice Co or a representative from Financial Advice Co has committed **serious fraud.**
  - Any other specified reportable situation that maybe introduced in the regulations.
- **Reportable situations** (other than investigations) in relation to another financial adviser or mortgage broker operating under a **third-party licensee**.

## How is a significant breach determined?

Financial Advice Co must report any breach or likely breach of a **core obligation** to ASIC where this is **significant.** Once the core obligations have been identified the law prescribes two ways for determining significance.

### Core obligations

Core obligations comprise of:

- Financial Advice Co’s general licensee obligations including:
  - do all things necessary to ensure that the financial services covered by the license are provided efficiently, honestly, and fairly
  - comply with the conditions on our licence
  - have adequate arrangements for managing conflicts of interest
  - have adequate resources (including financial, technological, and human resources) to provide the financial services covered by our licence and to carry out supervisory arrangements
  - be competent to provide the financial services covered by our licence
  - have trained and competent representatives
  - take reasonable steps to ensure that our representatives comply with the financial services laws
  - have a compliant internal dispute resolution procedure for retail clients
  - have adequate risk management systems
  - have compensation arrangements for retail clients
  - comply with ASIC reference checking and information sharing protocol, and
  - comply with certain financial services laws.
- Division 2 of Part 2 of the ASIC Act relating to unconscionable conduct and consumer protection.
- Other financial services laws, being Chapter 7 of the Corporations Act (financial services and markets), in addition to Chapters 5C, 5D, 6A, 6B, 6C, 6D and 9.
- Other specified Commonwealth legislation, in so far as they cover conduct relating to the provision of financial services, namely the Insurance Act 1973, Insurance Contracts Act 1984, Life Insurance Act 1995, Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Act 1993 and Superannuation (Resolution of Complaints) Act 1993.

The two ways for determining significance of core obligations are as follows:

### Deemed significant breaches

Breaches that are ‘deemed significant’ are taken as significant and no further analysis is required to determine whether the breach is significant or material, as no threshold assessment applies.

Deemed significant breaches are where:

- The provision breached is an offence for which conviction may be punishable by imprisonment for:
  - 3 months or more if the offence involves dishonesty
  - 12 months or more for any other provision.
- The provision breached is a civil penalty provision (includes the efficiently, honestly, and fairly and best interests obligations), unless it is excluded (see below).
- The provision breached is misleading and deceptive conduct provisions of the Corporations Act and/or ASIC Act.
- The breach results in or is likely to result in **material loss or damage** and the financial product or service is provided to a client (wholesale or retail client).
- Any other specified circumstances prescribed by regulations.

The Corporations Regulations excludes several breaches of civil penalty provisions from those items that are to be deemed significant, and so these do not need to be reported solely on the basis that they are a breach of a civil penalty provision. Whilst these individual breaches are excluded from automatic reporting, they must still be assessed according to the factors in the next section.

Refer to Appendix 2: What is a deemed significant breach.

### Other breaches that may be significant

Where a breach of a core obligation is not ‘deemed significant’, as outlined above, the breach must then be assessed by Financial Advice Co to determine if it is significant, and so a reportable situation arises. The factors that must be considered are:

- The number and frequency of similar previous breaches
- The impact of the breach or likely breach on Financial Advice Co’s ability to provide the financial services covered by its AFS licence
- The extent to which the breach or likely breach indicates that Financial Advice Co’s arrangements to ensure compliance with its obligations are inadequate
- Any other matters that may be prescribed in regulations.

Whilst Financial Advice Co must have regard to all the factors listed above when determining whether a breach (or likely breach) is significant, a breach may be significant even if only one of the factors applies to the circumstances, or when there is a combination of factors.

## Investigations that continue beyond 30 days

Investigations into whether a significant breach (or likely significant breach) of a core obligation has occurred, and that continue for more than 30 days, must be reported to ASIC as a ‘reportable situation’.

If the investigation commences and concludes within 30 days and there are no reasonable grounds to believe that a reportable situation has arisen, the investigation is not a ‘reportable situation’. If an investigation that concludes within 30 days identifies reasonable grounds to believe that a reportable situation has arisen, Financial Advice Co must inform ASIC of this reportable situation within 30 calendar days.

## Requirement to report on other licensees/ representatives

Where you have **reasonable grounds** to believe that a reportable situation (excluding an investigation) has arisen in relation to a financial adviser (or mortgage broker) from a third-party licensee, you must notify Financial Advice Co via the Adviser Portal within one business day. Financial Advice Co must report this to ASIC and the third-party licensee.

Protection is afforded under the law in relation to qualified privilege in action for defamation, assuming there is no malice or improper motive for lodging a report.

## Failure to lodge a report

Failure to lodge a report to ASIC and the other licensee may attract civil penalties.

## Investigation and notification obligations

Financial Advice Co must investigate breaches of the law where a reportable situation has occurred and a potential loss to the client is involved (and certain other criteria) and notify and remediate clients in certain circumstances. These obligations are summarised in the following table and incorporated into the Incident and Breach Assessment process as relevant.

| **Action** | **Financial Advice Co requirement** | **When** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Action 1: Notify affected clients of the breach of the law | Take reasonable steps to notify affected clients in writing of the breach | Within 30 days of knowing or becoming aware of required circumstances |
| Action 2: Investigate the breach | Start an investigation into the nature and full extent of the breach | Within 30 days of knowing or becoming aware of required circumstances |
| Action 3: Notify affected clients of the outcome of the investigation | Take reasonable steps to notify affected clients in writing of the outcome of the investigation | Within 10 days of the investigation concluding |
| Action 4: Remediate affected clients for the breach | If there is loss or damage and an enforceable right to recover, take reasonable steps to pay affected clients’ remediation of an amount equal to the loss or damage | Within 30 days of the investigation concluding |

*\*Authorised representatives will also be notified.*

### When do these obligations apply?

These obligations apply when Financial Advice Co knows, or should know whether the following circumstances exist:

- Personal advice is provided to a retail client, **and**
- There are **reasonable grounds to believe** that one of the following reportable situations has arisen:
  - Breach of a core obligation and the breach is significant.
  - Conduct constituting **gross negligence.**
  - **Serious fraud** has been committed.
  - Any other specified reportable situation that maybe introduced in the regulations, **and**
- Financial Advice Co has **reasonable grounds to suspect** that the client:
  - Has or will suffer a **loss or damage** because of the reportable situation (the loss or damage does not have to be material), and
  - Has a legally enforceable right to recover the **loss or damage** from Financial Advice Co.

## Financial Advice Co compliance requirements

### Authorised Representative Obligations

Financial Advice Co values reporting of possible breaches by advisers, their staff and Financial Advice Co staff and considers it an essential part of its compliance culture.

- Authorised Representatives are required to report any incident or suspected breach to Financial Advice Co within one business day of the event being identified, using the Incident Notification Form available on the Adviser Portal.
- If the Risk and Compliance Team requires further information or documentation, this request must be complied with immediately.
- Financial Advice Co Compliance will then review the issue, determine its classification, and decide on any appropriate actions to be completed; including, where it is classed as a reportable situation, notifying ASIC within 30 days.
- A formal investigation will be instigated where the matter involves personal advice to a retail client.
- Where appropriate, remedial action will then be implemented. This may include training, supervision or monitoring of Representatives (where the breach relates to their actions), refunds, reimbursements and/or compensation to clients and in some instances, revocation of authority (this is at the discretion of the Licensee).
- Financial Advice Co will communicate with the Authorised Representative throughout the above process.

### Breach & Incident Register

Financial Advice Co will centrally register and record incidents that relate to the licensee and all Authorised Representatives.

Staff and Authorised Representatives who identify and record the incident through the Adviser Portal will receive an email notification of the incident in confirmation. If you do not receive an email confirmation notification, within a 72-hour period, please contact the Compliance Team via e-mail or telephone. The compliance team will also advise the relevant adviser that an incident has been recorded and that the matter is being investigated.

## Non-compliance with this policy

The following ramifications to the licensee may result from non-compliance.

### Impact on Licensee

- Infringement notices - The licensee maybe subject to an infringement notice (in addition to the other enforcement options listed) for the following offences where Financial Advice Co fails to:
  - inform ASIC of reportable situations within required timeframes
  - inform ASIC of reportable situations in the prescribed form
  - lodge a breach report about a financial adviser or mortgage broker from another licensee, and/or
  - notify other third-party licensee of a breach report lodged about them.
- Civil and criminal penalties - Failing to lodge a reportable situation to ASIC when there are reasonable grounds to do so may attract both civil and criminal penalties. The maximum civil penalty for not reporting a reportable situation in accordance with the obligations as an AFS licensee is:
  - for an individual licensee—the greater of 5,000 penalty units, and three times the benefit derived, and detriment avoided; and
  - for a body corporate licensee—the greatest of 50,000 penalty units, three times the benefit derived, and detriment avoided, and 10% of the annual turnover for the 12-month period ending at the end of the month in which the body corporate contravened or began to contravene (capped at 2.5 million penalty units).

The value of a penalty unit is currently $222 for offences committed on or after 1 July 2020.

### Impact on Authorised Representatives

In addition to other specific measures referred to in this policy, non-compliance with this policy will be classified as an incident and will be dealt with as per the Breach and Incident Management Policy.

### Impact on Financial Advice Co management and staff

Non-compliance with this policy may result in disciplinary action and could include the termination of employee relationship with Financial Advice Co if the breach is considered serious.

If you are uncertain about how this policy applies to a particular circumstance, or you have any questions about the policy please contact [contact@financialadviceco.com.au](mailto:contact@financialadviceco.com.au).

## Appendix 2: What are deemed significant breaches

Deemed significant breaches include:

- Breaches that constitute the commission of an offence and the commission of the offence is punishable on conviction by a penalty that may include imprisonment for:
  - three months or more if the offence involves dishonesty; or
  - 12 months or more in any other case;
- Breaches of a civil penalty provision (if the provision is not exempted under the regulations);
- Breaches that contravene s1041H(1) of the Corporations Act or s12DA(1) of the Australian Securities and Investments Commission Act 2001 (ASIC Act) (misleading or deceptive conduct); or
- Breaches that result, or are likely to result, in material loss or damage to:
  - a person or persons to whom the AFS licensee or a representative of the licensee provides a financial product or a financial service as a wholesale or retail client;
  - in the case of a managed investment scheme—a member or members of the scheme; or
  - in the case of a superannuation entity—a member or members of the entity.
