## Purpose

The main objectives of this policy are to ensure that our Authorised Representatives understand:

- Expectations in relation to client reviews where a review service has been accepted by the client;
- The current requirements for client reviews;
- The “enhanced” FDS and consent requirements for Ongoing Fee Arrangements; and
- Documentation to be used when establishing and conducting reviews. This policy should be read in conjunction with the Basis for Charging Fees Policy.

## Expectations

### Expectations for Establishing a Review Service

- A mere **offer of a review** cannot be included as a service offering. If you have existing clients where this exists in your service agreement, then these must be adjusted to remove the offer and substitute it with a statement that a review **will be provided**, as well as the frequency at which it will be provided (e.g., once a year, twice a year, every quarter, etc).
- Authorised Representatives must consider whether a review service for a client is required and is in the client’s best interest, basing this judgement on the type of advice provided to that client and their circumstances. Where a review service has been accepted the expectation is that at least one comprehensive review will be provided within the agreement period.
- Where you have purchased a book of business from another Authorised Representative and a review service has been accepted you must ensure that you provide the review agreed to as part of the previous agreement.
- Authorised Representatives must inform their clients at the time the initial advice is provided whether a review service is recommended. An overview of the recommended service must be included in the advice document and a separate agreement provided to the client for signature with all of the details of the service(s) to be provided. The client should be informed of and understand the intent to review the advice and have an expectation of when and how often reviews will occur.
- Authorised Representatives must explain to the client the importance of a regular check of their financial strategies and portfolios, as those strategies, and the client circumstances, will likely need to change over time.
- In the event a client declines a recommended review service arrangement, you must:
  - warn the client of the implications of not having regular reviews and how these can be essential to helping the client achieve their financial objectives.
  - ensure that the advice is still appropriate in light of no review service arrangement being in place, before proceeding to implement your recommendations.
  - ensure that the client understands they will be responsible for reviewing and managing their portfolio moving forward, as that usually forms part of a review service arrangement.
  - communicate to the client if a product or platform cost will alter as a result of not having a review service arrangement in place.
  - make it clear to the client that they will have to engage you on an ad hoc basis, along with any costs that may involve, to manage their investments going forward or recommend a product solution that does not require adviser involvement.
- The fees charged must be commensurate with the level of service, whether that is a comprehensive review, or another service offering, such as quarterly portfolio rebalancing, monthly budgeting meetings, or other services.
- You are required to provide the client with the services promised within the time period set out in the agreement.

Approved service agreement templates have been provided, that must be used to establish service arrangements. Any alterations to these templates must have prior approval before use.

### Expectations when a Client Accepts a Review Service

- A review service must be properly documented in an agreement document. It is no longer appropriate to establish a review service arrangement in an SOA and have the client accept by signing the Authority to Proceed.
- Where a client does accept a review service arrangement, it is essential that you:
  - Document the elements of the review service that you are offering, together with the frequency and nature of the service.
  - Agree on the cost of the service, and document that agreement.
  - Monitor and keep track of your commitments so that you deliver the services agreed.
  - The outcome of a review must be documented in an approved Licensee SOA or ROA document template appropriate to the circumstances.

Note that a review service can include more than a single point in time review of the client's position. For example, reviewing your client’s portfolio and making recommendations, where required, or re-balancing at irregular intervals is also a valid service offering.

## Review principles

- The review process provides the opportunity for the client to reaffirm their position on advice previously provided and helps to keep the client fully informed of their current position.
- A review involves a check in with the client to review any changes to the clients personal and/or financial circumstances in order to consider whether any changes to the previously agreed strategies are required. The review will also consider changes in the legislative and economic environment. We would expect that a comprehensive review would include:
  - Updated information in the form of new/revised data collection documents and/or file notes
  - Reassessment of current strategies against current goals & objectives and legislative changes
  - Review of insurance needs, contributions and projections
  - Reassessment of risk profile and portfolio asset allocations
  - Consideration of current products against APL and research data
  - Confirmation of services and fees
- Frequently, clients may be seeking reassurance from an Authorised Representative about certain aspects of their existing investments, insurances etc. These interactions will quite often occur over the telephone. Where the client is seeking reassurance in relation to their personal circumstances, advice may be required. **All interactions**, whether general contact or more formal review meetings with your client, **must be documented in a file note** and retained in the client file. **Any advice** resulting from the interactions **must be recorded in an appropriate advice document**. Please refer to the Data Collection and Risk Profiling policy for more detail on updating and storing information.
- Reviews must occur at the frequency agreed with the client.

## When must a review be conducted?

A review must be conducted within the time frames as outlined and agreed to in the service agreement. The review timeframe can never be greater than 12 months regardless of the type of service arrangement entered into with the client.

Where you are aware that client availability might be an issue, for example your client travels often for extended periods of time, you should factor this into the timing of the review. If your client cannot be reached and the review is not conducted within the timeframe committed to in your service agreement, this will be a breach of your agreement and it will need to be terminated. In addition, if the services promised are not delivered within the specified time period, you **may** be required to refund fees (see “Fee for no service” later in this policy).

In the event that the client may not be able to physically attend a review meeting, consider conducting your review via other means, such as over the phone and video conference etc in order to meet your obligations. Note that if your client is overseas then the requirements of the Overseas Client Policy must be followed.

### Expectations for Non-Contactable/Disengaged Clients

- For reviews, if it is not possible to make contact with the client (where, for example, they are travelling) to arrange a review meeting, you may not be able to deliver the services agreed to (albeit out of your control). In order to provide the agreed review, you need to understand any changing needs of your client and if their relevant circumstances have changed (noting that your BID obligations apply to further advice as much as they do for initial advice).
- If you are able to communicate with your client via any means, e.g., email, phone call etc and can confirm their current situation and details of any relevant changes that have taken place, you can then provide your client with an advice document with any appropriate recommendations (even if it’s a hold/maintain recommendation). Your advice should also include how they are progressing towards achieving their goals and objectives, and recommending any changes required.
- If a client declines to receive the services agreed to, they are deemed to be a disengaged client and the service agreement must be terminated. You should explain to the client that as they are disengaged the review services will cease and no responsibility for portfolio management will exist from that date forward. In these circumstances fees for services not delivered within the period set out in the agreement must be refunded. You must notify Compliance by lodging an Incident Notification on your adviser portal if these circumstances arise. If your client is in a product or platform that can only be managed through an Adviser relationship then you must inform them of this and recommend that they make arrangements to move their funds to a product or platform that does not require an advice relationship. Any advice as to which product or platform would be suitable will constitute advice and require an advice document.
- Where a client is uncontactable, i.e., no response to emails, has changed address and can’t be located, has left the country for extended periods, or for other reasons, the review service should be terminated, and the fee may need to be refunded plus interest. You must notify Compliance by lodging an Incident Notification on your adviser portal if these circumstances arise.

## The review processes

A client review checklist is contained at Appendix 1. Use of this checklist is not mandatory provided the same level of information is contained in client records.

The following are Financial Advice Co requirements:

- The review frequency agreed with the client should be recorded in the review service agreement and kept on the client file.
- The relevant review date should be diarised in the Authorised Representative’s CRM.
- Prior to the client’s review date, the Authorised Representative should send the client a Review reminder, together with all relevant attachments, which are to be completed by the client and then returned to the Authorised Representative.
- In the review reminder, it is not appropriate for an Authorised Representative to insert a paragraph advising the client that if they do not respond to the letter, then the assumption is that the client does not want a review.
- Where the client fails to respond or indicates that they do not wish to proceed with the review, the Authorised Representative must contact the client to follow-up on the review offer. This contact can be made either by telephone, email or letter, based on communication methods agreed with the client. It is important to document this follow-up on the client file, i.e., keep a file note containing the contents of the telephone discussion or retain a copy of the email or letter to the client.
- Where the client, despite your best efforts does not attend or participate in a review meeting (either in person, electronically or by providing the required information and requesting the review in writing by mail/email) or is not contactable at the time the review is due, then the service arrangement must be terminated as a review cannot reasonably be conducted without taking into account the client’s circumstances.

For avoidance of doubt the following guidance is provided.

| **Circumstance** | **Outcome** |
| --- | --- |
| Client unable to come in for face to face meeting but discussion to ascertain if any change of circumstances has occurred electronically (e.g., Phone, email, video call), and outcome is fully documented. | Conduct the review based on the factors documented in discussion within the timeframe specified in the review service agreement. The agreement may continue. |
| Unable to contact client in any way. | Review cannot be undertaken within timeframe specified in the review service agreement. The agreement is to be terminated. Fees in respect of services not provided are to be refunded. |

If client still does not want to discuss or provide answers to questions then the review cannot be undertaken within the required timeframe. The agreement is to be terminated. Fees in respect of services not provided are to be refunded. |

Where some services, but not others have been delivered, reimbursement, where required should be made for the cost of the services not provided. For this reason, it is important that you understand and have documented the cost components of your service offering.

## Can I offer my clients a subscription service?

A subscription service is a service where you agree with your clients to provide general information on a regular agreed frequency. This type of service usually comprises information of a general nature (e.g., newsletters, podcasts, webinars and the like), which does not take into account any client’s personal circumstances, and is therefore considered general advice. As such this type of service offer would not be considered to be a service arrangement or an ongoing fee arrangement.

Every subscriber to the service would get the same information. The fees would need to be commensurate with the service.

It’s important to note, these are considered *ancillary services* by ASIC and ASIC has stated that in their experience these types of ancillary services are generally less valuable to a client than the review of the client’s overall financial situation. So, whilst a subscription service may be included in your review service agreement, it cannot be the only service offered under such an arrangement.

## Fees for No Service

Failure to deliver services that have been agreed with the client and charged for is a serious matter, which constitutes Fees for No Service, and will be reported to the Risk and Compliance Committee for further action. The onus is on you to advise Compliance by lodging an Incident Notification on your adviser portal if these circumstances arise and you believe any client may fall into this category. If you have any doubt, you must consult Compliance. Systemic failure to provide contracted services to clients will result in notification to ASIC, financial compensation to clients and cancellation of your authority.

## Annual Renewal of Ongoing Fee Arrangements (OFA)

### What is an Ongoing Fee Arrangement (OFA)?

From a regulatory perspective an OFA exists when:

- A Licensee or Representative gives Personal Financial Product advice to a retail client; and
- The client enters into an arrangement with the Licensee or Representative; and
- Under that arrangement a fee is to be paid for a period of more than 12 months.

From 1 July 2021, Financial Services providers that receive fees under an Ongoing Fee Arrangement (OFA) are required to:

- Provide clients with a single document (known as an “enhanced” FDS (EFDS)) each year outlining the fees and services that were delivered in the previous 12 months (as well as the services promised) and the fees that will be charged and the services the client will be entitled to in the following 12 months and seeks annual renewal from the client for all OFAs; and
- Obtain written consent before fees under an OFA can be deducted from a client’s financial product.

### Fixed Term Agreement

Fixed Term Agreements (FTA) are agreements entered into for a period of **12 months or less**. These agreements are permitted for use by ARs of Financial Advice Co AFSLs at present.

Under an FTA, as fees are to be paid for a period of less than 12 months, the EFDS/annual renewal requirements do not apply, however it is important to be aware of the following:

- Provided the FTA includes a fee that continues for exactly 12 months (or less), then terminates automatically, it is not an Ongoing Fee Arrangement and the “enhanced” FDS/annual renewal obligations do not apply.
- If a fee continues for more than 12 months (even by as little as one day), it is an Ongoing Fee Arrangement and the “enhanced” FDS/annual renewal obligations apply.

For example, if you have an FTA and your client is paying fees via a platform and the fee has not been set up to stop (i.e., there is no end date), it will constitute an Ongoing Fee Arrangement and the legislated annual EFDS requirements must be met. Likewise, a Direct Debit arrangement from a credit card or bank account that does not end at the end of the 12 months will also create an OFA.

You must not under any circumstances establish Fixed Term Agreements in an attempt to circumvent the law or try to avoid the annual EFDS requirements.

If using a Fixed Term Agreement:

- there must be robust process in place to ensure that the fees are terminated and do not continue for more than 12 months.

## “Enhanced” Fee Disclosure Statements (EFDS)

An “enhanced” Fee Disclosure Statement (EFDS) is a statement in writing that includes the information set out below:

For the previous 12-month period of an **Ongoing Fee Arrangement (OFA)**:

- The fees paid by a client over the last 12 months, up to and including the day before the Anniversary Date;
- The services promised to the client over the last 12 months; and
- The services provided to the client over the last 12 months.

For the next 12 month period of an **Ongoing Fee Arrangement (OFA)**:

- The actual or estimated fees a client will pay over the next 12 months;
- The services promised over the next 12 months;
- A renewal authorisation.

The fee recipient (i.e. you) must seek annual agreement from clients for fees to be collected from financial products (except where fees are charged to credit cards or basic banking accounts). The client’s consent to fees will need to be passed on to product providers annually.

The purpose of the “enhanced” FDS is to help clients understand the services they have paid for, the services they have received and how much those services cost, as well as being aware of the fees they are going to pay, and the services promised for those fees, for the following 12 months.

The required information, as well as the requirement for clients to renew their arrangements annually, is so that clients have *“more frequent opportunities to assess whether the services they were receiving were commensurate with the ongoing fees they were paying and provide greater visibility around the nature and amount of fees being deducted automatically from their accounts”.* (Explanatory Memorandum)

## Providing Fee Disclosure Statements

### Principles

- On an annual basis, all clients with whom an ongoing fee arrangement (OFA) exists, must receive an EFDS outlining the fees charged, the services available to them and the services actually received by them for the previous 12-month period, together with the services offered for the following 12 months and the fees the client is expected to pay.
- An EFDS must be provided to clients within **60 days** after their anniversary date.
- An Ongoing Fee arrangement exists where a Financial Advice Co AFSL or a representative of a Financial Advice Co AFSL gives personal advice to a retail client and the client enters into an arrangement, the terms of which provide for the payment of a fee during a period of **more than 12 months**.
- An Ongoing Fee arrangement is any fee (however described or structured) that is paid under the terms of an ongoing fee arrangement.
- Neither a Financial Advice Co AFSL or its authorised representatives can enter into a scheme for the sole or dominant purpose of avoiding EFDS obligations.
- The EFDS must contain information as set out above.
- EFDSs must be in writing and provided to clients in person, via email, via mail, or by any means as agreed by both the client and Financial Advice Co.
- A copy of any EFDS provided to a client must always be added to the client’s file/record and retained.
- An EFDS must always cover a period of 12 months before and after the anniversary date. Once set, the anniversary date cannot change, unless the service agreement is ceased and a new one commenced.
- An approved EFDS template has been provided by Financial Advice Co. Any variations to this approved template must be approved prior to use by Financial Advice Co.

If you fail to provide a client with an EFDS within the first **60 days** of the renewal period for the arrangement then you may be subject to a civil penalty. Repeated failure may constitute a systemic breach that may be reported to ASIC.

### Renewing ongoing fee arrangements

- An ongoing fee arrangement must be renewed every year.
- An “enhanced” FDS, which includes the consent to renew the OSA must be provided to the client no more than **60 days** after the anniversary date.
- The renewal period is **120 days from the anniversary date.** This means clients have 120 days from the anniversary date in which to renew their OFA. So, regardless of when you send the EFDS within the first 60 days of the anniversary date, the renewal period starts from the anniversary date, and **not** from the date you provide the EFDS. (Note: The 120 days includes the Anniversary Date itself.)

### Terminating Arrangements

- If no response is received within the renewal period (i.e., 120 days from the anniversary date), the agreement is taken to be ceased and the product provider must be notified within 10 business days from the end of the renewal period.
- A client’s consent ceases to have effect if the ongoing service agreement is terminated, or at the end of 30 days after the end of the first renewal period after consent is given for the OFA, i.e., the written consent will cease to have effect 150 days after the anniversary date if no response is received, and no further fees can be collected.
- At any time during the 120 day period a client may proactively choose to renew or not renew their ongoing service agreement. If they elect not to renew then the arrangement ceases immediately, and fees will need to be terminated and the product provider must be notified within 10 business days.
- If the client provides notification that they do not wish to renew, or a client does not complete the renewal process within 120 days of the anniversary date, then the ongoing service arrangement is taken to have terminated. If the client subsequently re-engages, a new agreement, which documents the new ongoing service offering and costs, must be prepared and signed by the client to replace the arrangement that has been terminated.
- During the renewal period, while waiting for the client response you should continue to undertake your standard service obligations to your clients if they fall due in the renewal period.

### Variations To Arrangements

- You may vary an arrangement during its term, however any changes to fees, terms or services will require prior, written confirmation from the client. Where you are charging fees from a product provider an updated Licensee consent form is required in addition to any product provider forms.

### Consent requirements

Clients must consent in writing before the deduction of fees under an OFA can commence to be deducted from a client’s account. This consent expires after 150 days from the anniversary date, and must be renewed for each year of the arrangement.

The consent requirements apply to deductions from all financial product accounts, with the exception of credit cards and basic deposit products.

- **Direct Debit/Credit Card Payments** – Consent to deduct fees is not required where the payment is being made via a credit card or an account that falls within the definition of a basic deposit product in section 761A of the Corporations Act (e.g., a bank account). As a matter of best practice, if you are obtaining consent to deduct fees from the other products that a client holds, it may be easier to also capture consent to deduct fees from credit cards and bank accounts (where relevant).
- Consent can be provided in writing or electronically.

Licensee consent forms are required in addition to any product provider consent forms where required.

### Product Provider Consent

Note that client consent to deduct fees is mandatory and must always be obtained and available for inspection when required. Fees cannot under any circumstances be charged, without provision of client consent.

Deducting, arranging to deduct, or accepting payment of, fees from your client’s account without obtaining written consent is subject to a civil penalty.

- Where the account to be debited is provided by a third-party account provider (e.g., a product provider), you must obtain written consent to arrange the deduction and provide a copy of that consent to the third party.
- The written fee consent that you provide to the client must contain a minimum level of information which is contained within the Licensee consent form. It is possible that the product provider may adopt their own process for accessing the consent which must also be recorded on the file for record keeping.
- Where the fee is paid through joint accounts, consent must be obtained **from all parties** (e.g., husband and wife). If consent is not received by all joint account holders the fee consent is not considered to be completed. If one party revokes consent, the arrangement ends, and if one party wishes to change the nature of the consent then it must be agreed by all in the joint account.
- Where consent is varied or terminated, it is a legal requirement that the product issuer is notified within 10 business days.
- It’s important to note that ongoing advice fees cannot be deducted from My Super products. Whilst one off advice fees can continue to be charged, specific consent requirements must be adhered to. Refer to the following link for further guidance [https://treasury.gov.au/consultation/c2020-48919g](https://treasury.gov.au/consultation/c2020-48919g)

### Termination of an Ongoing Fee Arrangement

If the client does not return the fee consent, renewal or terminates the arrangement the following steps must be taken:

- All fees must be turned off;
- Give written confirmation to the account holder within 10 business days that the service arrangement has terminated;
- Where fee consent was provided to a product provider, provide a copy of the notice of termination within 10 business days.

If the above steps are not completed within 10 business days you must contact Compliance for direction.

Where you initiate the termination of an ongoing fee arrangement you must provide your client with the notice period contained in your agreement. Obligations regarding notice to product providers must also be completed within 10 days.

### Record Keeping

ASIC Information Sheet 256 (INFO 256) documents the requirements for record keeping in relation to EFDS and Client Fee Consent:

Note that failure to comply with the record-keeping obligations for ongoing fee arrangements is a criminal offence: see section 962X(1).

**EFDS Requirements:**

You must keep the following records in relation to an EFDS for at least five years:

- each EFDS that you have given to your clients, including the date when the EFDS was given and how it was given (e.g., via email or text)
- any notification from a client to renew, not renew or terminate an ongoing fee arrangement, and the date on which the notification was given, and
- if an ongoing fee arrangement has been terminated, the date on which it terminated and the basis on which it terminated.

**Client consent requirements:**

You must keep the following records in relation to a written consent for at least five years:

- each written consent given to you by a client, including the date when the consent was given (e.g., if the client provides their written consent by signing a box on a webpage, you can keep a time-stamped screenshot of that page to evidence the client’s written consent)
- each notice to vary or withdraw a written consent given to you by a client, including:
  - the date you were given the notice, and
  - the date you confirmed you had received the notice.
- communications from you (e.g., emails, SMS and letters) about giving the third-party account provider:
  - a copy of the written consent
  - a copy of a notice to withdraw or vary the consent, and
  - written notice of when the consent ceases, and
- details of any arrangement between you and an account provider
- Records of electronic documents must also be kept. For example, you can keep an image of the SMS, the sender’s name and date of receipt.

Additionally, you must ensure that these records are accessible to your Licensee, through storing them in an approved CRM system. This is essential for your Licensee to allow them to monitor compliance with their OFA obligations.

From a procedural perspective:

- Record the EFDS anniversary dates in your Client Relationship Management (CRM) system.
- Record the date that reviews are conducted and track all other services you have promised to deliver in your CRM system.
- Maintain current contact details for clients within your CRM system to ensure successful delivery of the EFDS.
- If required, provide copies of review documents in the Financial Advice Co lodgement tracking system, available through the Financial Advice Co web site. Note if you do use an approved software package then details must be recorded in that system's CRM.
- Review the EFDS and ensure it is accurate prior to giving it to the client.

### Adviser absences

In the event that there is a review service agreement in place; and in circumstances where an adviser is away for an extended period and is unable to complete the agreed services then arrangements must be made for another AR to take over the servicing obligations during this period. The client should be advised of this change, and their agreement for the change in arrangements must be obtained and documented in a file note. In the event that another AR cannot be put in place to meet the servicing obligations then the review service agreement must be terminated.

### Good operating practice

- Generate the EFDS after the monthly revenue reporting periods (generally between the 1st and 8th of the month) to ensure all fee data is captured.
- In addition to the services outlined in your agreement with the client, track any additional services provided in your CRM for inclusion in the EFDS.
- Only offer clients a review service agreement where you believe it is in their best interests to do so.
- If there is a change in adviser as a result of an adviser terminating his/her authorisation with a Financial Advice Co AFSL, then if the service obligations are taken over by another AR under a Financial Advice Co AFSL the new AR must write to the client advising them of the changed arrangements.
- If you have taken over a client from a previous Authorised Representative or licensee you should enter into a new agreement with that client, if review services are required. Note that until you have made new arrangements you are still responsible for ensuring services agreed to by the client and former adviser are delivered as agreed.
- If the EFDS is returned to sender as a result of an incorrect address, contact the client to obtain a current address and ensure the EFDS is delivered (minimum 2 attempts; these must be documented). If the client has not responded to the delivery of the EFDS the arrangement will terminate at 120 days.

## Deceased estates

### Individually owned Assets

When an individual client dies, any review service agreement will terminate. Additionally, any existing Powers of Attorney automatically lapses on death and are no longer valid.

From the date of the client's death, you have no entitlement to deduct fees under any review service arrangements or to alter any portfolio or investments on behalf of the deceased client. Fees charged after the date of death may need to be refunded.

However, you can enter into a new advice engagement with the Executor of the estate. Note that as executor’s role is primarily to distribute the assets of the estate it is generally not appropriate to enter into a review service arrangement with the executor. You may instead enter into a fee for service arrangement.

### Jointly Owned Assets

Where there are assets owned as joint tenants, ownership of such assets automatically passes to the surviving joint owner(s) on the death of one of the owners. Accordingly, if assets under advice are jointly owned as joint tenants, and there is a joint review service agreement in place, then a new agreement may not need to be signed. If you have any doubt, please consult with Compliance.

Where assets are owned as tenants in common, and an owner dies, then that owner’s share of the asset remains an asset of their estate and is subject to the terms of the deceased’s will, and should be advised upon in the same manner as assets that are partially owned, as noted below.

Note that where the assets under advice are partially jointly owned and partially individually owned, then continuation of an existing service agreement is not possible due to the mixed ownership of the assets, and a new service agreement must be entered into with the executor and/or the surviving individual owners.

Until such time as a new agreement is entered into you have no entitlement to deduct fees under any previous service arrangements or to alter any portfolio or investments on behalf of the deceased client. Fees charged after the date of death may need to be refunded.

Examples:

- If a review service agreement is between an adviser and an individual client, the death of the client will cause the agreement to be automatically terminated.
- If a review service agreement is between an adviser and more than one client and there is a surviving client, you should check the terms of the agreement to confirm whether you are permitted to continue charging fees to the surviving client’s account.
- If a review service agreement is between an adviser and a company, the agreement continues even if a director who entered into the agreement on behalf of the company has died. This is because the company is treated as a separate 'legal person' from the director and survives the death of the director.
- If the client under the review service agreement is an individual Trustee, and the Trustee dies, then the agreement is automatically terminated.
- If the clients under the agreement are two or more individual Trustees, then the agreement can continue if there are one or more remaining Trustees.

### Continuity of Advice

As death will result in cessation of ongoing advice, then in the absence of other arrangements, you should swiftly offer to provide advice services on a fee-for-service basis to the Beneficiaries or Executor of the Estate, so that the assets of the estate continue to be properly managed in accordance with the wishes of the beneficiaries and executor.

### Timing of notification

Where there is a delay in the notification of death and date of death, review service agreements must terminate from date of death; not date of notification of death. In some instances, this may entail a refund to the estate. Where the beneficiaries or executor requires advice then a new fee arrangement must be agreed to.

### General obligations

When you receive notification of the death of your client(s) who have a review service agreement, you must take the following actions:

- File note all relevant details, including how and when you were notified and by whom. This may be in the form of a death certificate, a doctor’s certificate or advice from the beneficiaries or executor.
- Check the terms of the review service agreement to confirm whether you are entitled to continue charging advice fees to a surviving client.
- Where appropriate, discuss any required alteration of any terms of the review service agreement with any surviving joint client.
- Ensure that you have written evidence of notification to all product providers of the client’s death if there are no other parties to the ongoing fee arrangement, and request product providers to cease ongoing advice fees from the date of death.

## Gearing

Gearing is considered to be a circumstance where a client borrows money in order to invest. Accordingly gearing includes arrangements where funds are borrowed from banks, other financial institutions or individuals for the purposes of investing. This includes but is not limited to margin lending, investment loans or mortgage loans.

Where an Authorised Representative has provided gearing advice to a client, an appropriate **review service is mandatory**, and should be factored into fees charged. If a gearing client declines a review service, you should decline to implement and/or provide the advice. This is because of the inherent risks associated with gearing. A review of a client’s portfolio should include revisiting income and expenditure, assets and liabilities, personal circumstances, goals and attitude to risk. It is also important to review the client’s current LVR, investments and cash flow to service any outstanding debt.

It’s important to remember that you cannot charge asset based fees on geared portfolios; refer to the Gearing Policy for further guidance on this issue.

In the event it is evident that client engagement is not occurring then you should contact the client and advise them of the risks of maintaining a geared portfolio without them participating in a review, and consider terminating the arrangement.
