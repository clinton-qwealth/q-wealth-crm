## Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to provide Financial Advice Co Pty Ltd, its Authorised Representatives, employees and officers with a clear and consistent understanding of the legislative requirements and Financial Advice Co’s business requirements applicable to complaints management.

As a holder of Australian Financial Services Licenses (AFSLs), Financial Advice Co Pty Ltd has an obligation to comply with the regulatory requirements in relation to complaints management.

In order to meet our compliance obligations and to ensure that complaints are managed in an effective manner, our complaints management framework has been developed in accordance with our legislative and regulatory requirement and the recommendations in ASIC Regulatory Guide (RG) RG 271 Internal dispute resolution.

This policy has been developed to provide a framework for complaints management. The main objectives of this policy are to ensure that we have an effective complaints management framework in place to:

- **Acknowledge and record complaints** in a timely manner;
- **Assess and investigate complaints** to determine appropriate resolutions, which may include assessment of any appropriate compensation;
- **Monitor complaints** to track the progress of their resolution; and
- **Report complaints** to Responsible Managers, and the Financial Advice Co Pty Ltd.

Financial Advice Co Pty Ltd makes a firm commitment to each and every client to provide quality client service, research and advice and to ensuring Financial Advice Co Pty Ltd retain oversight of complaints received and to the efficient and effective resolution of complaints received in relation to financial product advice and services Financial Advice Co Pty Ltd offers.

The complaints handling process forms an integral part of Financial Advice Co Pty Ltd Compliance Program.

## Scope

## Definitions

### What is a Complaint

Financial Advice Co Pty Ltd adopts the Australian Standard definition of a complaint, namely *“An expression of dissatisfaction made to Financial Advice Co, related to its products or services, or the complaints handling process itself, where a response or resolution is explicitly or implicitly expected.”*

### Types of complaints

Complaints usually fall into two categories - service related or advice related. In either case the process to follow is the same, although the consequences and remedial actions, where a complaint is found in the client’s favour, may differ.

### Service-related complaints

A service-related complaint is one where the client complains about the way you conduct yourself or business. Examples of service-related complaints include (but not limited to):

- A client complains because you were late for an interview;
- A client complains saying you were rude;
- A client complains that you didn’t return their phone calls.
- A client complains of harassment, intimidation or bullying.

Service complaints do not include any complaints where the customer alleges they’ve suffered a financial disadvantage.

### Advice related complaints

An advice related complaint is one where the client complains about the advice they have been provided which has caused them to suffer a financial disadvantage; the client alleges that you have misled them about what to expect from their product; complains that you have made an administrative error that has caused a financial loss or complains that you have breached their privacy. Examples of an advice related complaint include (but not limited to):

- I was not told of fees and charges;
- I was told I’d get a return of 10%;
- I wasn’t told I could lose my money;
- I only wanted a short-term investment;
- I didn’t know my money was locked up;
- I’ve missed out on social security benefits because of your advice;
- My accountant told me that your recommendations have increased my tax assessment;
- You didn’t follow my instructions to apply dollar cost averaging and the market has gone up;
- You failed to advise me of the benefits I was losing by exiting my old insurance policy;
- You ticked the wrong box on my application form and you invested me into an incorrect investment;
- You didn’t get back to me for your promised 6-month review where we were going to invest the second half of my funds; and
- You disclosed my situation to my spouse / partner etc \[privacy issue\].

## Internal Complaints Handling Process

### Who can make a complaint?

Complaints can be made by any person or third parties on behalf of customers, or directly to the Authorised Representative. Where a complaint is received by a third party you need the complainant’s consent to discuss with the third party unless it is clear that the complainant has provided authority – for example where a complaint is lodged by a solicitor on behalf of a client or by another party (e.g., a new financial adviser) and the complainant is copied into the complaint notification and you verify that the contact details are correct. Best practice is to get the complainant’s consent in writing but this is not mandatory. A verbal complaint carries as much weight as a written complaint.

### Complaints made via social media

Where a complaint is made via a licensee’s or Authorised Representatives social media presence, then, provided the complainant is identifiable and contactable, that complaint is regarded as a complaint that must be managed in accordance with this policy. Note that complaints made on third party social media sites are not covered by this process. The table below provides examples:

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Complaint is made on Financial Advice Co Pty Ltd Facebook/LinkedIn/Twitter | Complainant is identifiable and contactable | Complaint must be managed in accordance with this policy |
| Complaint is made on Financial Advice Co Pty Ltd Facebook/LinkedIn/Twitter | Complainant is not identifiable and contactable | Complaint is not managed in accordance with this policy as complainant is not identifiable and contactable |
| Complaint is made on a community Facebook page | Complaint is not managed in accordance with this policy unless complainant is identifiable and contactable, and this is brought to our attention. |  |

## Complaints assessment and investigation

Each complaint will undergo an investigation which will be managed by the Complaints Manager. The purpose of the investigation is to analyse the validity of the complaint, assess any potential liability and recommend an appropriate resolution.

All complaints will be reviewed by at least the Responsible Managers of Financial Advice Co Pty Ltd. Findings of the assessment and investigation will be recorded in the CR.

Complaints will also be assessed to determine whether a potential or actual incident or breach has occurred. Where a potential or actual incident or breach has been identified, such matters will be reported in the Breaches and Incidents register and managed as per the Breach and Incident Management Policy.

## Complaints Triage

Once a complaint has been received it must be assessed and priority given to those cases that are considered urgent and severe. Examples of matters to be prioritised include where:

- the complainant is experiencing domestic or financial abuse;
- the complainant has a serious or terminal illness; or
- a delay in addressing the complaint could adversely affect the complainant’s basic living conditions.

## Internal Dispute Resolution

Internal dispute resolution (IDR) will be conducted by Financial Advice Co Pty Ltd complaints manager. Once a complaint is resolved by the IDR process the affected adviser is bound by that decision. The IDR process involves a review and assessment of the merits of the complaint against available information.

The types of remedies available may include, but are not limited to:

- A rejection of the complaint;
- Monetary compensation;
- A letter of apology; and
- Provision of free services (e.g., review of client’s file).

We will endeavour to resolve complaints within 5 business days. However, some complaints do take more time than others. If we anticipate that a complaint will take longer than 21 days to resolve, we will contact the complainant within this time to provide an update on progress. Should it take longer than 30 days, we will contact the complainant in writing to provide an explanation of the reason for the delay, and advise them that they may escalate the matter to AFCA.

Included in our response letter are the details of the external dispute resolution scheme available to the complainant should they disagree with the resolution offered. A complaint is considered closed when:

- The complainant accepts the resolution offered and any required remediation activities have been completed (e.g., compensation paid); or
- When there is no further dispute or response from the complainant for three months after the response letter was issued; or
- The complaint has been determined by External Dispute Resolution and no further action is required from Financial Advice Co.

## External disputes resolution

If a complainant does not feel that their complaint has been resolved in a satisfactory manner, or if a response has not been received after 30 days, a complaint can be lodged with the Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA).

AFCA provides fair and independent financial services complaint resolution that is free to consumers. AFCA accepts complaints in regards to either:

- a financial planning or advice matter;
- a credit assistance matter; or
- a superannuation matters

We will provide in writing details of how to access AFCA to all complainants.

If a client is not fully satisfied with our response to their complaint, they do have the right to take the complaint directly to AFCA. Contact details for AFCA are:

The Authorised Representative contribution will not exceed the excess payable for that claim.

## Cost recovery

Where complaint resolution is determined in favour of the client then the following cost recovery principles will apply:

- Where a complaint is submitted to Financial Advice Co’s PI Insurers then we will be bound by the decision of the insurer, although we reserve the right to recover the excess payable from the adviser.
- Where PI insurers are not involved, and where the adviser is at fault then the amount of compensation will be recovered from the adviser. This may be through deduction against earnings the adviser.
- Where Financial Advice Co Pty Ltd is at fault then Financial Advice Co Pty Ltd will bear the cost of the complaint.
- Where there is shared responsibility then Financial Advice Co Pty Ltd and the Adviser will negotiate an appropriate sharing of the cost.

## Complaints Monitoring and Reporting

All complaints received must be recorded in the CR. RM of Financial Advice Co Pty Ltd (or delegate) will monitor the CR on a regular basis to:

- Track progress of outstanding complaints; and
- Identify any recurring and systemic issues.

A complaints status report will be provided monthly to the Risk and Compliance Committee and Advisory Board. The report will include the following items:

- Status of complaints; and
- Information on claims paid.

## Reportable incidents

Systemic and significant issues identified from individual complaints through the complaints process may become reportable as an incident or breach depending on the severity. Please refer to the Breach and Incident Management Policy for further information on the breach and incident process.
