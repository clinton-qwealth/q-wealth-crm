## Background

According to the Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC), “the term ‘whistleblower’ has several meanings, but is usually used to refer to someone who alerts the authorities to misconduct within an organisation”. (ASIC Information Sheet 0052)

Advisers of Financial Advice Co (FAC), along with FAC directors, officers, contractors and employees, are committed to a culture of corporate compliance and ethical behaviour. This is demonstrated by the values and standards contained in the:

- FAC Code of Conduct which applies to all advisers
- Codes of Conduct applying to professional bodies that FAC subscribes to; in principal the Financial Planning Association, and the Association of Financial Advisers.

These are collectively referred to as the ‘FAC Codes of Conduct’.

An effective Whistleblower policy is considered by FAC to be integral to an effective corporate culture. As noted by ASIC: “Corporate cultures of silence, which allow wrong doing to go undetected, are seen as contributing to the recent round of local and international corporate failures. A regime protecting whistleblowers is seen as part of the answer because it encourages reporting of contraventions by employees.” (ASIC Paper entitled “Whistleblowers: Company officers’ obligations.”)

Specifically, our expectation is that an effective Whistleblower Protection Policy will, amongst other things:

- Help promote internal accountability and improved morale within FAC by providing appropriate confidential avenues for potential Whistleblowers to express their concerns;
- Expose wrongdoing and Improper Conduct (which includes corruption and fraud), before the problems identified become a public issue with the adverse publicity, legal costs and the potential for reputational damage to FAC that may follow;
- Result in more effective compliance with all relevant standards, codes and laws, and
- Provide a clear demonstration that we take our corporate governance obligations seriously. “Under the Criminal Code of the Commonwealth, a company can be convicted of criminal offences which have an ‘intent’ element. This means that a conviction can result if it is established that a company had a culture that directed or encouraged, tolerated or led to non-compliance, or that the body failed to maintain a culture that required compliance with relevant legislation.” (ASIC Paper entitled “Whistleblowers: Company officers’ obligations”).

For these reasons, FAC directors appreciate the significance of being able to demonstrate an open, transparent and compliant culture.

## Purpose of this Policy

The purpose of this policy is to provide FAC and its advisers and staff with a clear and consistent understanding of the legislative requirements and FAC business requirements applicable to whistleblowing. This policy reflects that position.

This policy therefore:

- Establishes an appropriate system designed to facilitate the reporting of Improper Conduct;
- Makes provision for relevant matters to be investigated by a committee set up for that purpose, and the resultant recommendations to be reported to the Board; and
- Protects those reporting Improper Conduct from being discouraged to speak out because of the fear of repercussions and/or reprisals. In particular, FAC is committed to ensuring that any person reporting Improper Conduct is not disadvantaged or penalised by being dismissed, demoted, harassed or otherwise discriminated against.

## To whom does this policy apply?

This policy applies to all FAC directors, officers, employees, advisers and contractors.

## Who may invoke the Whistleblower Protection Policy

Whilst any person may “blow the whistle”, in order to obtain legislative protection as a Whistleblower, ASIC Info 52 lists certain criteria that must be met, and certain things that must be done. These are:

| **Criteria** | **Requirement** |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Your role | You **must** be: a current officer (usually that means a director or secretary) of the company your disclosure is about; a current employee of the company your disclosure is about, or; a contractor, or the employee of a contractor, who has a current contract to supply goods or services to the company your disclosure is about. |
| 2. Who the disclosure is made to | You **must** make your disclosure to: the company’s auditor, or a member of the company’s audit team; a director, secretary or senior manager of the company; a person authorised by the company to receive whistleblower disclosures, or; ASIC. |
| 3. Providing your name | You **must** give your name to the person or authority you are making the disclosure to. |
| 4. Reasonable grounds to suspect breach | You **must** have reasonable grounds to suspect that the information you are disclosing indicates that the company or company officer may have breached the Corporations Act or the *Australian Securities and Investments Commission Act 2001* (ASIC Act). |
| 5. Made in good faith | You **must** make the disclosure in ‘good faith’. That means your disclosure must be honest and genuine, and motivated by wanting to disclose misconduct. Your disclosure will not be ‘in good faith’ if you have any other secret or unrelated reason for making the disclosure. |

## Objectives

In line with the Australian Standard on Whistleblower Protection Programs for Entities (AS 8004 – 2003), the objectives of this Policy are to:

- Encourage and facilitate the reporting of matters that may cause financial or non-financial loss or reputational harm to FAC;
- Provide avenues for those who have legitimate concerns to raise those concerns freely, confidentially and without fear of reprisal;
- Provide a system complete with the appointment of personnel that will allow reported matters to be reviewed and investigated (by a specific committee set up for this purpose), with the appropriate recommendations to be made to the FAC Board and such remedial action taken as appropriate; and
- Set up guidelines concerning the support and protection of individuals making disclosures of Improper Conduct.

## Process

### The Reporting System

Examples of the types of concerns where it may be appropriate to use the Whistleblower mechanism include:

- Serious breaches of the FAC Codes of Conduct and/or other instances of illegal or unethical conduct;
- Evidence of fraud, dishonesty, corruption or other unlawful conduct on the part of FAC directors, officers, employees, contractors or advisers;
- Instances of miscarriage of justice in the context of, (for example), unfair dismissal;
- Claims of bullying;
- Health and safety risks within the workplace;
- Other conduct that may cause financial or non-financial loss or otherwise cause damage to the reputation or financial viability of FAC.

Where any person who believes they have an issue that needs being dealt with under this policy they should report it to the group MD. Both parties have obligations for ensuring confidentiality.

Where the matter relates to a person’s employment, FAC Human Resources Department has existing procedures in place designed to enable any employee to lodge a grievance relating to their current employment. The Whistleblower Protection Policy is intended to cover major concerns that fall outside the scope of such procedures.

- Whistleblower reports can be made anonymously. However, legislative protections may not apply to reports made anonymously. Anonymous Whistleblower reports will be reviewed to decide whether or not the matter should be the subject of further investigation.
- In all cases, FAC will take all reasonable steps to protect the identity of the Whistleblower, and will adhere to any statutory requirements concerning the confidentiality of reports made. FAC will also ensure all documents and files created in relation to a report of Improper Conduct, whether paper or electronic, are kept secure.
- In appropriate cases, disclosure of the Whistleblower, or the allegation made by such Whistleblower, may be unavoidable, as in the case where a report in terms of this Policy culminates in court proceedings, disclosure is required by the Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC), the Federal Police or under the State or Commonwealth laws etc.
- Subsequent to receipt of Whistleblower reports FAC has an obligation to examine the immediate welfare and protection needs of the relevant Whistleblower(s), if identified.

### How FAC will respond

- In order to protect individuals and those accused of misconduct, initial enquiries will be made to decide whether an investigation is appropriate, and, if so, what form it should take.
- Some concerns may be resolved after discussions with the Whistleblower, without the need for further investigation.
- Within ten working days of a concern being raised, a written response will be provided to the Whistleblower, acknowledging that the concern/issue has been received and indicating how it is proposed to deal with the matter. ### Investigation

- Once a report of suspected Improper Conduct has been received from an individual who provides reasonable grounds for his/her belief that Improper Conduct has occurred, in appropriate cases, a meeting may be convened to investigate the matter.
- The composition of the investigative committee will vary depending on the nature of the conduct to be investigated. However, in each case the committee should involve a minimum of three or more members (or in some cases their duly authorised delegates), including, but not limited to:
  - Group General Manager – Advice
  - Head of Group Legal/external legal
  - Company Secretary
  - Financial Controller
  - Chief Information Officer (Information Technology)
  - Human Resources Manager/external HR expertise
- Where an investigation concerns a FAC director, officer or employee above the rank of a Senior Executive member, no person will be permitted to delegate their function/role to anyone else.
- A quorum of three (3) members will be required to investigate any given matter. Failure to obtain a quorum in any given case will require that an investigative meeting be reconvened.
- In certain cases, there may be a need to refer the relevant report to an appropriate external investigator. (Such external investigator may include external lawyers and/or accountants or other specialist third party as appropriate).
- A matter will not be investigated by any person who is either implicated in the subject matter of the Whistleblower’s report and/or who is known to have a direct influence over the person investigated.
- The key functions of such investigations are to:
  - Collate information relating to the allegation as quickly as possible;
  - Consider the information collected;
  - Make a recommendation based on the evidence in a manner that is fair, objective and affords natural justice to all parties involved;
  - Make contemporaneous notes of all discussions, phone calls and tapes of all interviews with witnesses when conducting an investigation;
  - Ensure confidentiality, including, but not limited to, establishing a confidential filing system for storing all information it has received, conducting interviews in private and taking all reasonable steps to protect the identity of the Whistleblower;
  - Warn the Whistleblower where disclosure of the identity of the Whistleblower cannot be avoided due to the nature of the allegations;
- Any witness may have legal or other representation or support during an interview. If a witness has a special need for legal representation or support, permission should be granted. Costs associated with legal or other representative will be the responsibility of the party engaging that representation.
- In conducting any investigations, an Investigation Plan will facilitate the listing of issues to be substantiated and consider the avenues available for investigating the alleged improper conduct made against the FAC. For sample Investigation Plan, see Annexure C attached. See also Annexure D attached for pro-forma file note for recording the formal interview of the FAC member under investigation for improper conduct.
- At the conclusion of the investigation, a report will be prepared setting out findings. These findings/recommendations will be presented to the FAC Board via the FAC Compliance Committee. See Annexure E attached for sample Final Report format.
- Where an investigation shows that wrongdoing has occurred within the company, FAC is committed to changing its processes and taking action in relation to those FAC employees who have behaved incorrectly.
- Where illegal conduct may have occurred, this may involve reporting the matter to relevant authorities and, in some cases, to the police.
- In some circumstances FAC may also be required to report instances of Improper Conduct to the relevant regulatory authorities, such as ASIC.

## Whistleblower Protection

- A Whistleblower who reports matters in good faith will not be penalised, or personally disadvantaged because he or she has reported the Improper Conduct in question. In particular, FAC will not tolerate any instances of a legitimate Whistleblower being:
  - Dismissed;
  - Demoted;
  - Subjected to any form of bullying or persecution; or
  - Otherwise discriminated against.
- If a legitimate Whistleblower subsequently reports being disadvantaged for speaking out, that report will be assessed as a new report of Improper Conduct and will be investigated on the basis set out in this Policy.

## Rights and Responsibilities of Whistleblowers

- Whistleblowers must in all cases:
  - Act in good faith when making a complaint and not make a false disclosure; and
  - Assist with the investigation of the complaint to the extent required.
- The intentional filing of a false report is itself considered Improper Conduct which FAC has the right to act on. The making of any deliberately false or malicious allegations by a representative of FAC is a serious offence which may result in a disciplinary action up to and including dismissal.
- Whistleblowers should not attempt to obtain evidence they have no right to access. Such improper access may itself be considered Improper Conduct.
- Whistleblowers making a report of alleged Improper Conduct should be prepared to set out all known information regarding any reported allegations and provide evidence in support of any such allegations. (See Annexure A attached for details of the information required when lodging an Improper Conduct – Incident Notification Form). In this case Whistleblowers may be entitled to request positive action by FAC to protect them during an investigation where it will not be possible to maintain the anonymity of the Whistleblower.
- Anonymous Whistleblowers must provide sufficient corroborating evidence to justify the commencement of an investigation.
- As anonymous Whistleblowers are unable to be interviewed, it may be more difficult to evaluate the credibility of the allegations and therefore, less likely to cause an investigation to be initiated.
- Where possible, and assuming the identity of the Whistleblower is known, the Whistleblower will be kept informed of the outcome of the investigation of his or her report, subject to privacy and confidentiality considerations.
- Protection of a Whistleblower’s identity will be maintained to the extent possible within the legitimate needs of the law.
- Legislation allows FAC to pass on the identity and information provided by a Whistleblower to ASIC, the Australian Prudential Regulation Authority and the Australian Federal Police without asking for permission from the Whistleblower.
- The Act does not allow further disclosure of a Whistleblower’s identity to be made without their consent. For example, the FAC Company Secretary cannot pass on the Whistleblower’s disclosure to members of the FAC Board or, say, the Chief Executive Officer of FAC unless the Whistleblower has consented to them doing so.

## Training in Whistleblower Protection Policy

Training programs (at the induction level and ongoing training) will be devised for staff and advisers concerning the importance of reporting improper conduct practices that may arise from their day-to-day work, procedures for such reporting and also practical examples on how to avoid these.

## Non-compliance with this policy

Non-compliance with this Policy may result in disciplinary and/or legal action, and could include dismissal, or termination of a relationship with FAC, if the breach is considered serious.
