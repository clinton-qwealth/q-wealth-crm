## Context

As an Authorised Representative of a Financial Advice Co licence, you are licensed to provide the financial services under our AFSL listed on your Authorisation Certificate.

However, many of our Authorised Representatives also act in different and separate capacities e.g. as accountants, or work co-operatively in joint ventures or referral relationships with businesses who operate in these capacities.

This policy addresses the implications and potential conflicts of interest that can arise when an Authorised Representative’s conduct is associated with other business activities in conjunction with their financial services business.

## Risks

The main risks that can arise from participating in other business activities in conjunction with your financial services business are:

- Your client may be unable to clearly distinguish between your activities as an Authorised Representative of Financial Advice Co licence and any external activity;
- Privacy issues that may arise if there are common databases and staff across each of the businesses.

## Separation is essential

It is essential that distinct lines of separation are maintained between each business and the capacity in which they operate. This is to ensure that you minimise any liability/risk to you, your practice and Financial Advice Co licence that might arise if you were to engage in activities (or were perceived as doing so) for which you are not authorised and for which you are not covered by Financial Advice Co Professional Indemnity cover.

## External business activities that are not permitted

Even where the external business activity is conducted by another entity, Representatives of a Financial Advice Co licence are not permitted to operate in the following areas:

- Representative of another AFSL holder without Financial Advice Co express authorisation under a Deed of Cross Endorsement;
- Property sales/development/buyer’s agent (except where you are investing your own funds). Under no circumstances must you invite any third party (client or otherwise) to participate in an activity of this nature or conducted by you;
- Promoter of any investment scheme (i.e. unregistered managed investment schemes; for example, horse racing syndicates, property investment schemes); and
- Any other business activity which has the potential to compromise the reputation of Financial Advice Co licence and/or create an unacceptable conflict of interest with regard to your financial planning relationship with your client(s).

## Separation of business requirements

Where you are engaged in, or are associated with, non-financial planning activities in addition to your financial planning activities, these must be conducted as separate businesses.

This must be done in such a way that clients clearly understand in which capacity you are operating in when you are providing a financial service (versus non-Financial Planning activities) and the scope of your activities and responsibilities in that capacity.

This is to be achieved through the separation of corporate entities:

- Financial Planning, and non-Financial Planning businesses must be conducted through separate corporate entities. There may be unique circumstances where this may not be practical. In those rare instances, explicit written consent to operate in this manner must be obtained from the Financial Advice Co compliance team at [contact@financialadviceco.com.au](mailto:contact@financialadviceco.com.au). Approval is not guaranteed, and will only be approved where the Compliance Team is satisfied that:
  - There are unique reasons why the entities cannot be separated; and
  - There are robust measures in place to ensure that clients are not confused as to whom they are dealing with.

As well as the separation of corporate presence:

- Separate email, web pages and social media presence.
- Separate marketing and promotional material.
- Separate stationery and branding.
- Separation of files and client documentation.

## Professional indemnity insurance

As an Authorised Representative of Financial Advice Co, the fees you pay us include provision of Professional Indemnity cover for activities authorised by us, under our AFSL.

Any activities that fall outside of the scope of your authorisation by Financial Advice Co are not insured under your financial planning cover. Professional indemnity insurance cover must therefore be maintained for all business activities external to the Financial Planning business.

Prior to finalising appointment as an Authorised Representative of Financial Advice Co, evidence of current Professional Indemnity for related businesses must be provided to Financial Advice Co.

## Stationery, signage and promotional material

Any non-Financial Planning business must have separate identification and promotional material. If you wish to include references to both the Financial Planning and Non-Financial Planning businesses in promotional material you must seek approval from your Compliance Team.

## Explanation of distinction to clients

You must ensure clients are informed of, and clearly understand, the delineation between the advice and services provided as an Authorised Representative of Financial Advice Co, and those provided in any other capacity.

The distinction must be explained in discussions with the client and accurately reflected in all promotional material (e.g. brochures) and advice documentation.

## Non-financial planning advice and activity

You must ensure that no non-Financial Planning advice (e.g. accounting, mortgage broking) is given within a financial planning advice document (either SOA or ROA) or in any document utilised for Financial Planning activities, including emails.

This also means you are not permitted to add, combine or attach any such documents (including client facing documents) to financial planning documents.

Additionally, you must not utilise any capacities inferred on you by your position in a non-Financial Planning business in your capacity as an Authorised Representative of our licence.

## Client files

Separate client files and databases must be maintained for each business. Where records are held electronically, the records relating to the different businesses must be stored separately with access restricted appropriately so that only those operating within the scope of Financial Advice Co AFSL, and your Authorisation can access that information. The only time that client files can be accessed is if there is written client consent to allow this.

## Confidentiality of client information

Client confidentiality must be observed. Client information obtained by one business cannot be disclosed to another without written authority from the client.

## Separation of business income

The non-Financial Planning business must not be used as a vehicle for the redirection of income away from the licensee. All income relating to the provision of financial planning advice within the scope of your agreement with the licensee must be paid to us.

Similarly, non-Financial Planning activities cannot be charged under the Financial Planning licence nor included within quotes or invoices for Financial Planning to ensure the client clearly understands which activities are within the Financial Planning authorisation scope.

## Conflicts of interest

The Financial Adviser Standards and Ethics Authority (FASEA) has published guidance in relation to Conflicts of Interest. FASEA standard 3, in particular is relevant and says *“You must not advise, refer or act in any other manner where you have a conflict of interest or duty”.*

Management of this matter is particularly pertinent where you have interests and associations with a related business (e.g. tax, accounting, SMSF administration, and the like). Such interests and associations will **always** be an actual conflict of interest.

Please refer to the Referrals policy on how to deal with conflicts of interest. However, in summary, when making or receiving a referral where there is an actual or potential conflict of interest you must be able to show that acting on that referral is demonstrably in your client’s best interests. This requires a higher level of evidence than simply being in the client’s best interests. You must document in your client’s file:

- Reasons why this arrangement is preferable to other reasonable alternatives.
- Evidence of explanation of other alternatives and why they are not suitable.

In addition, as part of your best interest duty obligations, you must always prioritise the interests of your client where a conflict exists between you and/or your business and your client.
