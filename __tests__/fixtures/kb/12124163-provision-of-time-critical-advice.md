## Purpose

Financial Advice Co is committed to providing high quality advice to our clients. We have an obligation as an Australian Financial Services Licensee to ensure that our Authorised Representatives comply with the legal and regulatory requirements in relation to providing quality advice to our clients. As a Licensee we have an overarching obligation to ensure that financial services are provided efficiently, honestly and fairly.

This policy provides guidance as to when and what time critical advice may be provided, and what the requirements are when providing time critical advice.

## What is time critical advice?

Generally, when personal financial advice is provided to a retail client a SOA must be provided to the client at the same time or as soon as practicable after the advice is provided, and before you provide a further financial service to the client (such as arranging for a financial product to be issued to the client) that is connected with, or is in relation to, the advice provided.

In practice this means that you may discuss your advice with your client and at the end of the meeting provide your client with the SOA which documents the details of your advice. Only then may you proceed with arranging for a financial product to be issued to the client.

The time critical provisions allow for the further financial service to be provided to the client before a SOA is provided under certain circumstances as documented in s946C(3) of the Corporations Act.

## What conditions must be met before providing time critical advice?

### What are the requirements?

There are **two elements** that have to be satisfied before time critical advice can be provided, **both of which must be met**. These are:

- The **client must expressly instruct you** that the further financial service must be implemented immediately or by a specified time; **and**
- **It is not reasonably practical** to provide the client with a SOA before the further financial service is provided.

### What does reasonably practical mean?

There is no legislatively defined definition of what “reasonably practical” means. Therefore, an informed judgement must be made to determine if a SOA can be prepared before the further financial service is provided to the client.

Generally, “reasonably practical” will be determined by the circumstances. The following are examples (not exhaustive) of where it might be considered NOT reasonably practical to prepare a SOA prior to provision of the further financial service.

- To implement participation in a corporate action with a short acceptance period.
- To implement advice in circumstances where your client is seeking advice promptly prior to him/her leaving the country or obtaining medical treatment.
- To assist clients obtain advice in the lead up to changed regulatory or product arrangements with a short implementation timeline.

Where an adviser has an ongoing service arrangement with a client, the Licensee expects the adviser to prepare advice in a timely manner to meet routine end of financial year and calendar year deadlines and legislative changes commencing at the beginning of the next financial or calendar year. For these deadlines, time critical advice should only be provided in extenuating circumstances.

## Time critical documentation

When you provide time critical advice, you must, at the time of providing the advice, provide your client with a time critical advice letter which must contain the following information:

- Information about remuneration (including commission) or other benefits payable \[s947B(2)(d)\]
- Information about interests and associations that would normally be provided in a SOA \[s947B(2)(e)\]
- Any warnings relevant to the advice (e.g. incomplete information) \[s947C(2)(f)\]
- Replacement product advice (if relevant) \[s947D\]

## FSGs

The normal requirement is to provide clients with a FSG as soon as it becomes apparent that a financial service will be or is likely to be provided.

Where you have provided time critical advice and it has not been reasonably practical to provide the client with a FSG prior to the financial services being provided, then a FSG must be provided to the client within 5 business days after the advice has been provided.

## When must the SOA be provided?

When you have provided time critical advice a SOA must be provided:

- Where no financial product cooling off periods apply - no later than 5 business days after the provision of the further financial service.
- Where a cooling off period in respect of a financial product applies then the statement of advice must be provided at the earlier of the start of the cooling off period or 5 days of the date of issuing the product.

## Advice Principles

The normal advice principles apply when providing time critical advice. This means:

- You must consider your client’s attitude to risk.
- You must consider their relevant circumstances by completing a fact find.
- You must consider whether the advice is appropriate to meeting the client’s goals, needs and objectives.
- You must only provide advice where it is in your client’s best interests.

Additionally:

- You must document your client’s express instruction to provide the further financial service immediately or within a specified timeframe.
- You must document why it is not reasonably practical to provide a SOA.
- You must lodge your time critical advice and subsequent SOA through the lodgment checklist process.
